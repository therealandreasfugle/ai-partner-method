## Pricing & Value Framing

### Pricing Architecture & Financial Modeling

Price dramatically affects the path to revenue targets—a $100k offer requires only 10 sales to hit $1M/month, while lower price points require exponentially more volume, making pricing strategy crucial to scaling speed.

> if you had a hundred thousand dollar offer you only need ten people to buy in order to hit a million a month

Calculate true cost per qualified lead, not cost per message; if 80% of 50 leads are unqualified, your real cost is $50/qualified lead, not $10/lead—don't use comparison bias to fear higher qualified lead costs.

Calculate net ROAS by deducting advertising cost, agency fee, sales commissions, and COGS from gross ROAS—this determines if the entire marketing approach is actually worth executing.

AOV (average order value) must exceed your cost per purchase to be profitable; if cost per purchase is $100 and AOV is only $50, you're losing $50 per customer acquired.

The higher your AOV from ticket sales, the more ad spend you can justify and sustain profitably, directly increasing the total size and revenue of the event.

Set collected dollar per book call targets at 4-5 times your cost per call; this ensures your call economics work across different funnel types and offer prices.

For long-cycle funnels (4-5 weeks to convert), expect volatile short-term ROI but strong long-term ROI (12:1 to 19:1); use Facebook invoicing or net-30 terms to avoid cash flow issues while fronting ad spend.

As you scale spend and reach colder audiences (from in-market to 'needs convinced'), expect ROAS to decline naturally because you're converting less-qualified prospects—this is not a sign something is broken.

Even if ROAS drops from 20:1 to 6:1 or from $2 CPL to $17 CPL as you scale, if unit economics remain positive, continue scaling.

When an agency proposes a budget figure with no financial model backing it, challenge them to show the math—if $20K won't hit your KPI targets based on current performance, it won't work, and the model will prove it before you waste money.

Budget requirements vary by funnel type: webinar funnels typically require 3-4x more ad spend than call funnels to generate sufficient data and reach profitability.

### Tiered & Dynamic Pricing Strategies

Use a tiered pricing strategy across the 60-day cycle: early bird ($37-$47 general, $97-$150 VIP for 4 weeks), extended early bird ($87-$97 general, $150-$200 VIP for 3 weeks), and punisher pricing ($147 general, $200-$250 VIP for final week).

> Early bird pricing is your cheapest pricing that you're typically going to have. Early bird can typically range from about 47. You can even go as low as 37 if you wanted to, but I like 47.

When transitioning from early bird to extended early bird pricing, open new audience segments on ads to avoid price anchor bias from previous pricing, which minimizes the typical sales drop from price increases.

Display crossed-out pricing to show the actual standard price during each promotional phase—this builds trust and proves you're not misleading when you later raise prices to the shown level.

Set timer on pricing boxes to countdown until the current price ends (not until the event starts), as this drives more urgency and FOMO than timing to the actual event.

Expect 30-50% of buyers to choose VIP pricing if the offer is strong enough, and use this percentage to calculate and increase overall AOV.

Structure your DM setting to uncover their exact monthly income target first, then qualify based on whether they can afford your offer price (with financing options), creating a clear qualification gate.

Use ultra-specific positioning statements combining niche targeting and unique mechanism (e.g., 'I help inconsistent traders between 1-3K per month with [mechanism]') instead of broad claims.

For a six-month program where clients will make $35K total, price between $3,500-$6,000, with $5,000 being optimal for balancing perceived value with market fit.

Price your offer at $2,997 as a starting point when you're new to the info space; calculate pricing by estimating total client revenue generation over the program length, then divide by 10 as a rough rule of thumb.

> I'm going to be charging $2,997.. $3k to learn a multi-six-figure high income skill.. 3k USD perfect starting point.

Structure pricing with three tiers ($4k DIY, $8k done-with-you, $10k done-for-you) so the gap between lower and middle tier appears larger, making the middle tier the most attractive anchor option.

Use sliding commission structures where commission rates increase by 1% or more increments as reps hit higher cash collection thresholds, creating ongoing incentive for additional revenue.

Price the high-ticket tier at $8,500/month (or test starting there) with the pitch being just an extra $120-150/month more than core tier for weekly 1-on-1s, positioning it as a small incremental investment for proven results.

### Value-Based & Outcome-Based Pricing

Frame value in terms of results multiplied by probability of success: target customers with higher baseline revenue so that even modest percentage improvements (e.g., 10%) generate significant absolute gains and justify higher price points.

> If I make 10% for a guy that makes 100 km he makes a measly fucking 10 km month, right? That still pays for the offer easily. However, if I didn't help make a guy who's already had a million a month and extra 10% in that month, he makes an extra 100 grand a month.

Price your offer proportionate to the financial outcome you deliver—if you teach someone to make $100k/month, charging $10k is justified; if teaching hot dog sales, charge only hundreds.

Calculate transformation value by mapping month-by-month income growth during the engagement period (e.g., $3K → $5K → $8K → $12K → $15K over 6 months = $30-40K transformational value) to justify premium pricing.

> how much money they expected to make. It's still not a month, one to month two.. What happens in the transformational value can easily be 30 to 40,000

Position pricing based on outcome logic—if your offer teaches how to make $50K/month, a $9K price tag makes sense because ROI is 5x. For beginners who won't hit that in 3-4 months, lower pricing is appropriate.

Use transmission of value: estimate realistic results clients will achieve (e.g., $40-50k over 6 months = $6.3k/month average) to justify your price point and plan future increases as credibility builds.

Divide your Transferational Value (TV) by 10 as a baseline pricing strategy; this universal rule has been tested and works across coaching businesses.

Calculate 'Transformational Value' (TV) by estimating total money prospects will make in the duration of your program; this becomes the foundation for all pricing decisions.

### Discounting & Financing Strategies

Offer financing cautiously and only as a secondary option; avoid aggressively pushing payment plans as it creates debt for underqualified buyers.

Offer financing or payment plans (e.g., pay-in-installments) to lower perceived barrier to entry without discounting, which increases LTV and closes more deals.

Pitch PIFFs (pay-in-full discounts) during sales calls instead of direct-to-checkout for high-ticket items, then offer payment plans as secondary option to maximize AOV and cash position.

Offering multiple payment options (pay-in-full + 3-5 payment plans) reduces the 'no' decision from 50% to 25% and increases yes likelihood to 75% by eliminating binary yes/no choice.

> if I introduce let's say the pay-in-full the payment plans and I have like three options for my payment plans.. That reduces the no significantly

When DMing or pitching to affluent prospects, anchor to 6-figure opportunity numbers (e.g., 'capture an extra $500k/month') rather than low 5-figure amounts, as it's more credible and attention-getting to wealthy audiences.

Offer multiple price tiers ($300/month or $2,000/year) to capture different buying preferences; most buyers opted for the monthly subscription.

Offer payment plans (e.g., $1,750 x 2 or $1,250 x 3) to remove cash-flow barriers for prospects who can't pay upfront.

Use specific price points like 11k instead of round numbers like 10k because it makes people stop and think 'why 11k', creating psychological engagement with your offer.

Price products below what they're worth to ensure affordability for your market, even if you could charge more—this maintains conviction and provides excess value delivery.

### Scarcity, Exclusivity & Premium Positioning

As your offer reaches exclusivity, the psychological perception of value increases; customers feel privileged to be selected rather than simply purchasing, which justifies premium and continuously rising prices.

> when you get the opportunity to go and eat there you feel like blessed you know like you're like oh my god I can't believe I get to go to carbon

Raise prices on finite, limited offerings (like masterminds with venue capacity limits) as demand increases—higher prices attract better-qualified, wealthier clients who can implement better and stay longer.

Use scarcity and hard caps as leverage to increase pricing over time rather than maintaining low prices indefinitely—raise prices incrementally as you reach capacity limits.

Create perceived scarcity by limiting access windows or forcing choice constraints (e.g., specific time slots only, limited seating); this increases perceived value and allows premium pricing.

Raise prices continuously as demand outpaces supply; the exclusivity components naturally justify price increases without needing additional marketing or sales efforts.

Only implement exclusivity tactics after you've reached near-maximum capacity in your current offer; exclusivity creates leverage to raise prices when demand exceeds supply.

> You've got to get to the point where you have damn near a full pipeline and you're close to being maxed out. That's the key. Once you get close to being maxed out, that is when you officially hold the car.

Implement a genuine hard cap on membership and communicate it clearly—don't use false scarcity tactics that destroy trust when you're inevitably forced to lift the cap.

Every time that you feel maxed out, instead of just immediately releasing all of your guardrails, use that position of being maxed out as leverage to get paid more money and reinforce the scarcity.

Show margin expansion from scarcity-driven pricing—when you cap supply and raise price, profit-per-customer increases dramatically (e.g., $1.5K to $10K = $8.5K/month difference).

> There's an 8.5K a month difference. That is tens of thousands of dollars extra per year. High tens of thousands of dollars per year between a 1.5K a month member and a 10K a month member.

Never offer certainty or guarantees to wealthy buyers; instead emphasize risk and tested strategies, as rich people expect and respect honest risk disclosure.

High-priced customers create better customer behavior—at $5k, 100% of customers log in and consume your content; at $500, only ~50% even log in.

Use price as a demand management lever; continuously raise pricing to slow growth at a rate you can service while filtering for higher-quality buyers.

Raise your prices significantly (2-3x) and reduce volume—even with a 10% lower close rate, you'll make more money per deal and have more time to focus on each client.

The type of customer you attract at the entry price point directly determines whether they'll buy higher-priced upsells; a $7 entry price attracts customers unlikely to spend $100-$300 later.

### Positioning & Messaging Strategy

Treat messaging exhaustion like resource depletion: some messaging works like oil fields (finite, will run dry) while others work like aquifers fed by springs (replenish over time at a certain rate); pull back spend to test which model applies before abandoning messaging.

Never offer high-ticket packages (e.g., $7K-$10K) without a premium visual aesthetic; use professional backgrounds (city skylines, proper lighting), high-quality graphics, brand colors, and carousel designs to signal that you're serious and making money.

> I cannot be in my mom's basement and my background is let's say my closet of clothes that's not properly folded and I have dirty clothes on me or I don't have proper lighting and I'm offering a seven-k package. It doesn't work that way.

Demonstrate perceived value consistency—if you charge high ticket prices, all your reels and page content must look high-ticket; avoid posting 10-20 reels in a row with the same setting, lighting, and clothes as this diminishes premium positioning.

Focus messaging on transformation outcomes and emotional pain points rather than features; tell clients 'you're overworked, not happy, can't get a girlfriend' and show them becoming 'a better man' instead of listing calorie tracking features.

Offer structure for high-ticket mastermind: include twice-monthly 30-min 1-on-1s, weekly group calls, recorded vault of master classes, quarterly in-person masterminds (4x/year with 100-200 attendees), active group chat, 30+ SOPs, and unlimited AI tool access.

Position your offer correctly by understanding your ICP's revenue level—the primary ICP is coaches making $10-50k/month doing everything themselves; secondary ICP is entrepreneurs entering coaching; adjust messaging and positioning for each segment.

Show prospects why they want their desired outcome by going deeper than surface-level goals; understand what the outcome actually gives them (confidence, lifestyle, security, reassurance) and use that in messaging.

For B2B coaching, build a balanced content mix of lifestyle (subtle, indirect), personality, and education—lead with value, let lifestyle back it up indirectly, so clients buy for transformation not just the flex.

Position yourself as the expert immediately after establishing the pain by showcasing your credibility assets, trust-driven content, client testimonials, and unique mechanism.

Charge premium pricing by solving specific pain points within your niche, not generic outcomes—the more specific the problem you address, the higher the perceived value and price point.

> Do you see the difference between talking about struggling high ticket closures versus a specific brand of high ticket closures in a specific market with specific pain points? Simply the end setting, it's so different.

The bigger the pain point you sell, the bigger the price you can command; frame your offer around the specific pain you solve to justify premium pricing.

Position yourself as the face of your brand to command higher upfront fees—coaches will pay $10,000+ upfront when they see you as the operator rather than a background figure.

When attracting a specific income bracket, state the target income level in your copy (e.g., '$20,000 per month') to filter for the right audience, as stating lower numbers attracts people below that threshold.

Position yourself as a specialist solving one specific problem deeply rather than a generalist solving many problems shallowly; specialists command premium pricing and higher margins.

Position your offer based on the client's current income level and what they need next; someone at $20k/month needs SOPs and delegation (not freedom), so reframe your solution around those pain points.

Messaging is the foundation of your offer; without clear messaging aligned to your client, you cannot package what your client is looking for.

Proper marketing is identifying your exact avatar and ideal customer profile (ICP), then demonstrating you have the value to help them; this enables premium pricing.

### Testimonials, Social Proof & Trust Building

Use realistic, relatable testimonials (e.g., someone doing $200K/month adding $178K in a week and a half) rather than only audacious best-case results, because leads seek worst-case scenarios to understand how badly things could go wrong.

Address objections and skepticism directly in your marketing by showing refund rates, dispute rates, case studies, and even testimonials from people who didn't succeed—transparency builds trust with skeptical buyers.

> we don't pick just the best testimony. We pick like we actually try to get people who refund or dispute to make a fucking video and explain.

Create a transparent breakout video showing your dispute rate, refund rate, and refund circumstances to address the common concern of 'how badly could this go wrong'; people prioritize worst-case scenario research over positive reviews.

Create breakout videos showcasing objective trust signals like refund rates, dispute rates, and transaction volume from your payment processor to build credibility before the call.

Show the complete transformation journey (not just the finished product) through case studies and testimonials so they can see themselves in the journey and believe it's achievable.

Frame your proof through transformational results with specific metrics (e.g., '$900/month to $4,800/month in 6 days') rather than generic statements about client success.

Use case studies of downgrade or refund situations in your due diligence emails to show that you prioritize customer success over keeping money, which builds credibility.

Openly state your income and financial targets without fear—if you're confident in your trajectory, speaking your numbers aloud is a conviction signal that shows you believe in achieving the next level.

Emphasize paid-in-full payment options and personally model the behavior by making large purchases yourself to demonstrate credibility and build trust with prospects expecting full payment.

> I did a poll inside of my inner circle group, which at a minimum, people have to make them vetted by the way. We literally look. Ask direct financial qualification: 'Do you have [specific amount]?' rather than vague income ranges, and structure income range options to match your actual audience (e.g., don't ask 0-10K, 10-50K ranges for a 100K+ minimum group).

> I'm very direct. I typically literally just tell people the price. I say this costs this much. Do you have this amount of money, okay?

Target the unsold middle demographic (people with swaying opinions) rather than focusing only on true believers, as they represent the largest pool of convertible buyers and are most influenced by messaging.

For prospects needing funding, create content showing the math, justifying the debt, and demonstrating refund/dispute rates before sales calls so objections are handled pre-call.

Target messaging to specific demographics based on how they'll fund the purchase: communicate to people with good credit if they need funding, or to liquid wealthy people if you want cash purchases.

Position DM closes at high price points ($2k-$5k+) without sales calls by using middle-funnel content that pre-qualifies leads and establishes belief in your mechanism before any direct selling.

Attract B2B clients with high-ticket offers ($3k-$40k+) by positioning through credibility and proof rather than direct offer copy; build trust first so buyers qualify themselves before contacting you.

For prices $7,500 or less, use direct-to-checkout; for $7,500+, use book-a-call with deposit to pre-qualify serious buyers and reduce no-shows.

Rich customers have higher financial endurance, faster implementation, better outcomes, and require less support—they're better customers despite higher acquisition costs.

Start with lower pricing on recurring offers when you first launch, then incrementally raise prices as your certainty, results, and system improve.

### AI Tools & SaaS Pricing

Price agentic AI tools with a credit-based model rather than flat-rate subscription, as it aligns incentives for thoroughness and allows customers to consume only what they use.

Offer tiered pricing starting at $20/month and scaling to $200/month with proportionally increasing credit allotments, making the tool accessible while allowing power users to scale spending with usage.

Agentic AI has higher token costs than pure chat/phone functions because it makes multiple API calls to external systems; price accordingly to account for increased computational costs.

> Here's the difference between costly and expensive. Costly is worth the cost of whatever it was, but it was a fair amount of money. Expensive is something that cost a lot where the quality was poor and therefore the product betrayed you.

### Payment & Subscription Frequency

Align your billing frequency with the speed at which you can deliver results—if you charge monthly, you must deliver meaningful results within 30 days to justify renewal.

Use recency bias to your advantage—ensure the most recent period before renewal (days 21-30) shows strong value delivery, because clients judge renewal decisions based primarily on what happened most recently, not the full program history.

You must exceed the value of what customers paid within the 30-day period before the next charge comes—track the specific metric of value you deliver.

### Profit Margin & Business Structure

For a $20-30k/month info business, target at least 66% profit margin minimum; for $100k+/month, target 70%+ profit; keep team costs to max 20-25% of revenue, with ads typically 5%, closers 10%, ops 5%.

Info products have 80-95% profit margins because you're acquiring leads through free social media, requiring zero fulfillment costs, making this one of the highest-margin business models available.

Info business is built for cash flow and profit, not for scaling team size; don't compare your spending to traditional companies with hundreds of employees.

Optimize for time at scale over profit margin: 70% profit margin at $100K/month working 4-5 hours beats 95% profit margin at $20K/month working 12 hours.

Consider tax-efficient business structure setup; locating your business in a low-tax jurisdiction can dramatically improve net profitability and reinvestment capacity.
