## Content Structure

### Instagram Story Highlights & Sequences

Build Instagram story highlights in five categories—Value, Expertise, Story, Clients, and Lifestyle—that together establish trust, credibility, and buying intent; value highlights provide free resources, expertise shows your knowledge, story demonstrates relatability, clients provide social proof, and lifestyle conveys authority.

> "I always tell my clients always to have one story that's going to be primarily based off of value.. My story, the reason why I did my story is because when I'm going to get followers coming in from my ads, especially when I do the profile funnel ad, I want to make sure that people resonate with my story."

Implement a weekly Instagram Stories posting schedule with specific content types: Monday = client results with DM CTA, Tuesday = value/pain points, Wednesday = personal story, Thursday = personal wins/payment screenshots, Friday = soft CTA for free resource, Saturday-Sunday = creative freedom.

> "The weekly Stories Structure. Monday, we want to post a client result with a direct call to action to DM you.. Tuesday, Provide value and help them understand why they haven't achieved their goal yet."

Structure educational stories to explicitly ask prospects to identify which problem they have (development, risk management, setups, etc.) so you can send a specific asset that solves that specific problem.

> "I want them to explicitly tell me what problem they have whether it's developing a trading plan risk management high probability setups or pro firm rules.. each and specific problem will have a specific asset that will get sent out that will do the conversion by solving the specific problem with a specific solution"

Create a personal transformation story highlight that shows a before state (pain point), the struggle, and the journey to results; use specific pain triggers relevant to your ICP (e.g., 'I had acne, couldn't look in the mirror, felt insecure') then explain the solution process.

> "Your mind story should be something like I'm now able to have a stick back. I walk around a 7% body fat. I feel confident.. But let's take a step back. 18 months ago, I was in the work strip of my life. I had acne all over my face. I couldn't even look at myself in the mirror."

Use a filter mechanism in value-driven stories by directly calling out the exact ICP situations (e.g., 'lost money on courses, can't trade consistently, blow funded accounts') to repel non-ideal clients and attract qualified ones.

> "lost money on multiple trading courses can seem to take consistently profitable know the theory keep blowing fun that accounts this the filter mechanism and the reason why is because I'm calling the direct ICPs problem.. this filters our non ideal client profile and allows us to have conversations with the exact kind of prospects we want"

In personal/nurture stories, showcase discipline and self-care (gym, meal prep, fashion) to build character-based trust since prospects judge credibility on appearance and personal habits before seeing your actual offer.

> "if I see someone that takes care of this physique he becomes trustworthy if I see someone that looks good dressed as well he has a very strong personality.. when you are in shape it needs you to have discipline and when you have discipline people can rely on you"

Vary your story sequences by intent (credibility, education, lifestyle, value, CTA) across different days to avoid exhausting your audience—rotate the types of stories rather than posting the same CTAs repeatedly.

> "Monday, you post a credibility sequence.. Tuesday, you post an educational.. Wednesday, you do a lifestyle.. Thursday, you do a value CTA.. Friday, you're back to credibility. Notice how you're not exhausting it."

Mix high-converting Stories with strategic variation—don't post the same type of content repetitively or audiences will ignore Stories predictably; rotate between wins, value, stories, and CTAs so viewers anticipate and click every Story.

> "if you're constantly doing cold actions or anything repetitively. Even if it's posting pseudon wins, if it's repetitive, people will start ignoring your stories because they can predict what is coming"

Keep your audience guessing with varied story content so they feel compelled to click—alternating between direct CTAs, value, personal stories, and social proof keeps viewers engaged rather than tuning out predictable patterns.

> "There's some people you look on Instagram, you see the story and you're like, yo, you feel like you need to click on it. There's some people you look at it and you're like, I'm not going to click on it because I know exactly what it is. The person where you feel like you have to click on it because you can't tell what it's going to be. That person is getting more leads."

Create a 'main story' sequence (20+ slides) that introduces who you are, where you came from, your obstacles, and how you got to where you are—make it relatable to your current ideal client's situation.

> "My story slides, which like I said, is a whole majority basically talks about everything, because imagine while you have someone that comes in.. it's like 20 slides of like who Wiley Barnett is and how he got to where he is right now."

Structure credibility stories by showcasing mentee's past situation (relatable problem) and current situation (proof of transformation) to make prospects see themselves in the results.

> "If I'm a future prospect that I see a current mentee's name is Mo and when he started to get 3 K per month blowing funded accounts, if Andrew is able to explicitly call that out and I listen and hear that. I mind. I think this person is exactly where I am."

Create story sequences with three components: value (quick wins), education (authority-backed teaching), and transformation (credibility-building results) to nurture leads through the middle of funnel before conversion.

> "The first step about story sequences is we can provide value to our audience. That's the most important thing. The second thing is education.. The last one is transformational."

In value-driven stories, showcase your transformation process step-by-step (week 1-5, week 6-10, week 11-14) so prospects emotionally imagine themselves going through the transformation.

> "the reason why I do this is because if I align with most position I now see myself emotionally going through this kind of transformation and if I'm able to understand how Andrew is able to help me and I'm able to see the different days and what's done on every single day I become so much more interest"

Post story sequences every single day consistently; the compound effect works where each daily story converts approximately 1% of cold audiences, reaching 100% conversion over 100 days.

> "a story sequence every day Historians one percent so when you do it for a hundred days People follow your story and they just want to buy it.. It's compound effect"

### Instagram Reels Strategy & Categories

Organize Instagram Reels into five categories—Educational (middle/bottom funnel), Credibility (middle funnel), Attention & Authority (top funnel), Personal & Reliability (top funnel), and Value (middle funnel with strong CTA = bottom funnel)—to move viewers down the sales funnel with increasing buying intent.

> "The categories are educational, which is a middle of funnel and bottom of funnel. There's credibility, which acts as a middle of funnel, there's attention and authority, which is top of funnel, personal and reliability. That is mostly top of funnel and there's value."

Structure Instagram Reel scripts with: Idea (what/why), Snap Judgment (background/setup), Title (what viewers will learn), Hook (results + wow factor), Body (chronological value in simple words with fast cuts), Final Statement (call to action or takeaway).

> "the idea obviously. So it's like, what is the overall idea? What is the video and why aren't you.. snap judgment is okay.. the title.. the hook.. the body.. the final statement"

Top of funnel content creates three psychological triggers: desire (dream outcomes/lifestyle), relatability (personal connection), and differentiation (uniqueness), while middle of funnel content changes beliefs, creates problem awareness, and pre-handles objections.

> "Top of funnel content works because it's designed to trigger three things in the viewer and all these three things are necessary if you want someone to follow you stay interested and eventually buy from you. Number one, it creates desire."

Understand the difference between dream follower content (broad, gets reach/engagement, builds audience) and ICP content (specific, nurtures existing audience, converts)—use dream follower content in growth seasons and ICP content to nurture and sell.

> "If you only post content for that specific person, the algorithm has nowhere to push it to. Your dream follower content creates the distribution surface that makes your ICP specific content discoverable. The dream follower is the ocean itself. Your ICP is the fish."

Create different video types for different funnel stages: top-of-funnel awareness videos (20-30K views), middle-funnel educational/full-course breakdowns, bottom-funnel in-person client collaboration videos that build relatability and trust.

> "Some are created for top of the funnel awareness that can't a decent amount of views for top of the funnel. Right? You can see that here. 12. This one is 20. Now, you can see vlogs of full courses break down everybody."

Segment YouTube content into four main categories: Educational (problem-solution), Credibility (testimonials and case studies), Personal (lifestyle and storytelling), and Personal/Value (narrative-driven content that builds connection).

> "We have educational, we have credibility, we have personal, and that's pretty much what I would do."

Diversify your reel content across different themes (credibility, lifestyle, personal values, education, POV) so your page showcases expertise, lifestyle, personality, and results—not repetitive content in the same outfit/angle.

> "The diversification is number one. Guys, do I have any posts back to back in the exact same outfit and same angle and same everything? I try not to have this.. In the actual content, bro."

Create content by sitting with clients in-person for 1-hour sessions; this forces higher output quality and eliminates procrastination; one session yields 1 YouTube video, multiple short-form clips, and story sequences.

> "I sit down with a client in person and the point is you force yourself to do it right from the start because when you're in an environment with other people, you'll never want to feel like you're losing."

Film Instagram Reels in short 1-2 sentence chunks at a time rather than one long take—this produces better audio consistency, cleaner edits, and more professional-sounding final videos with faster cuts.

> "Film one to do sentences until you're just like maxed out until you mess up. And you do that until it's perfect and then film the next one to do it. And then it's like you're at your self and you just cut it up and it will sound 10 times better"

Track which reels bring engaged, in-market followers (not just vanity metrics)—analyze follower growth, comments, saves, and engagement source to identify which content the algorithm favors and is pushing to your ideal clients.

> "Look at the followers and non-follower engagement. To see whether or not the algorithm is favorite in one of my real.. Meaning the algorithm is pushing it to people that don't follow my account. You want that."

Pin your highest-converting carousel or reel to the top of your profile—this should be your transformation/results post that hits all the pillars (credibility, lifestyle, results, process) in one asset.

> "The pin to care is still, which is essentially the transformation of how I made my clients over $4 million as a growth consultant.. right away when people go on your page, it just results."

Create four types of Reels: credibility-driven (your result), value-driven (actionable tip), educational (step-by-step how-to), and transformation (client's before/after)—each serves a different stage of the funnel.

> "credibility driven..Value driven and educational driven..Transformation, how Dylan's killed from 20 km on to 150 km per month. Educational is, this is how I run my 150 km of web design, agency"

Use a mixed content diet on Instagram: insights (what you learned), authenticity (vulnerable stories), lifestyle (day-to-day), relatability (funny/business content)—rotate these daily to avoid predictable content.

> "We have the what I'm constantly providing for my audience on my reels we have insights so what have I learned recently about info business which I can teach to my audience. We have authenticity. We have lifestyle. We have relatability"

Replace simple talking-head videos with third-person or side-angle camera setups to increase perceived quality and authority, especially when featuring clients or testimonials beside you.

> "Why not set up your camera in a third person get a side angle.. It increases your quality so much more. It increases your authority so much more."

### YouTube Long-Form Content Strategy

Create case study videos with this structure: (1) open with the prospect's original situation and pain points that mirror your audience's, (2) show step-by-step process (1-5 steps) of transformation, (3) show desired outcome achieved, ensuring it's visualizable and straightforward.

> "The beginning of the video would be the situation he was out in the past and what he was experiencing. And make sure you also vocalize the same pain points your audience currently has. And desired outcome is where you got your client. So from A to B and everything along the way will be step by step process of how you got it there."

Use a three-channel customer journey: Instagram Reels (front-end for attention), Instagram Stories (daily nurture sequences for belief-shifting), and YouTube (back-end for maximum conversion through case studies, objection handling, and value provision).

> "Imagine a customer journey and how one they are when they consume your story highlights, your credibility, lifestyle, value, authority, everything. So reels, educational, credibility, value, lifestyle, nurture, the care results, the stories, and finally, you push them on YouTube for the maximum conversion."

Build YouTube content around four formats only: Rosset-downs (build connection/person), Vlogs (sell lifestyle/identity), Value talking-heads (build authority/brain), and Case studies (prove results/proof) to answer all three buying questions.

> "when you rotate these four consistently you cover every reason someone would say yes and naturally again eliminate every single reason that they would possibly ever say no"

Direct audiences from short-form content (reels, stories) to long-form content (YouTube)—long-form builds mirror neurons and activates the prefrontal cortex, making prospects perceive you as a trusted mentor after hours of exposure.

> "When a person starts to listen to you for hours, their brain becomes the process you as the trusted friend or mentor.. Mirror neurons.. create a sense of shared experience."

YouTube is bottom-of-funnel content that builds deep connection; Instagram is top/middle funnel for reach. YouTube viewers become buyers because they've consumed 7+ hours of your content.

> "Instagram is where you track your leads. Very top of funnel, middle of funnel.. YouTube is bottom of funnel content. Instagram peeps the curiosity, whereas YouTube nurtures and builds a connection."

YouTube content creates longevity and converts at the top, middle, and bottom of funnel by providing in-depth value that Instagram (3-minute maximum) cannot deliver; it's essential for high-ticket B2B and info product sales.

> "if you don't have a youtube channel and you're in a fitness space or you're in the biz-op space or you have an info products for high value b2b You're stupid because how do you plan on providing so much value as I do better in only three minutes"

Target dream followers (broader audience in your general space) on Instagram Reels for reach and exposure, then use YouTube to filter and nurture the actual ICP with higher-intent content, creating a top-of-funnel to bottom-of-funnel pipeline.

> "Instagram Reels, we are targeting our Dream Follower, not our IPP.. Inside of our Dream Follower, we have our IPP, which with our IPP, we are nurturing them on YouTube."

Build a three-platform content system: Instagram Reels targeting dream followers for exposure, a micro-niche YouTube channel targeting ICP for high-intent leads, and Instagram Stories for nurture and conversion.

> "Instagram, you want to be focusing on getting exposure. YouTube, you want to be focusing on getting high intent leads, okay? If you just have exposure by itself though, it's not good. If you just have high intent leads, it is good, but with exposure, it's 10 times better."

YouTube is your primary monetization channel—post high-intent, original content with clear positioning on who you're targeting, why they should watch, and how you're different from competitors.

> "YouTube is a crucial part of my contentism. It is the biggest part. Like if I could post on Instagram or YouTube, I would choose YouTube. My Macroney's YouTube channel. I only have 14 videos that I have made. And I have made multiple six figures off of this YouTube channel from upfront payments alone."

YouTube content that generates sales should include: client interviews, case study breakdowns (using whiteboard software), and belief-shifting videos that contradict your own popular positions to capture attention.

> "YouTube videos that actually generate sales number one, there's three. Okay, number one is the client interviews.. case study breakdowns.. breaking limiting beliefs"

Create long-form case study interviews on YouTube (vs. short Instagram stories) featuring students with detailed results and step-by-step breakdowns because they seem more legit and allow deeper audience connection.

> "we have the shorter type of case studies over here. These are, you know, up to a minute long because that's all the Instagram story we'll allow you to do. But over here on YouTube, we can post a whole, you know, however long video we want of this interview. So it's just seems more legit to the consumer"

Post 1-2 YouTube videos during the 24-day launch period to nurture existing leads (people on the fence), drive outside traffic, and provide longer-form case studies that feel more legitimate.

> "we want to be nurturing people with YouTube videos. So drop one to two YouTube videos here with CTAs to the mentorship inside of the videos because you'll get outside traffic coming here to get extra leads but you will also nurture existing leads"

Post long-form vlog-style YouTube videos (20-40 minutes) during launch showing your daily life and lifestyle details so unfamiliar audiences can know, like, and trust you faster before they apply.

> "maybe it's 20, 30, 40 minutes long of you living your daily life. The audience is going to be able to know you like you and trust you just way more compared to not averaging this video"

Create YouTube content addressing specific, granular problems your audience faces (e.g., 'not tracking KPIs' rather than 'not working hard enough') by putting yourself in their shoes at their current income level.

> "Put yourself in their position put yourself in their shoes bro.. back when you were making one k per month.. what were your problems.. 'not working hard enough is too vague bro it's way too fucking vague."

Create educational content with a specific problem-solution format: pick one narrow problem (not broad topics), provide a specific solution, and deliver an unexpected angle or insight that shifts the prospect's mindset.

> "Problem, solution, I want you to pick a specific problem.. you have to pick a specific topic with a specific problem for specific solution"

Create problem-solution videos with extremely specific problems rather than broad topics, as audiences are more likely to engage with and assume better solutions when the problem is narrowly defined.

> "When you have a specific video with a specific problem, you can assume that the solution will be so much better, because it's so neat that it's so specific compared to a broad topic."

Don't exclusively post bottom-of-funnel content even though it converts; maintain a healthy mix of top/middle/bottom funnel to keep your channel sustainable and give bottom funnel content more leverage to convert.

> "Just because your bottom of funnel pieces of content are the ones that end up converting most of the time. Do not get into the trap of just posting bottom of funnel content."

Create middle funnel content as valuable, polished work you're genuinely proud of—don't just post videos to check a box, as poor content reduces repeat viewers and CTR on future videos.

> "YouTube videos are honestly I look at them as a work of art. You have to put the time in to make a decent YouTube video."

Use analogies to make niche-specific concepts accessible to broader audiences—instead of explaining funnel mechanics with jargon, use relatable metaphors like a leaky bucket to help 3 billion people understand what only 200,000 could before.

> "we're using an analogy so instead of 200,000 people, 3 billion people can understand it"

Use visual aids (whiteboards, graphics) to prevent cognitive load—when viewers must imagine what you're describing without visuals, they work harder mentally and disengage; show them visually instead.

> "when you visually can see something, they'll be able to relate with that topic, which means it increases pretension"

Your first 4-5 YouTube videos should be pure case study content showing results, not value teaching—extract multiple angles from each case study (e.g., 'How we did a $30k webinar' from a $100k month).

> "make content based on results.. your first like four or five videos should be like dropping just sauce case studies"

Post case study content on YouTube and repurpose with Instagram daily posts—old YouTube videos from 5+ months ago will resurface and drive booked calls as people discover your content library.

> "Post on YouTube and post on Instagram just just drive these people to watching your YouTube"

### Content Pillars & Positioning Framework

Build content around three core pillars: Pillar 1 sells the person (authenticity/values), Pillar 2 sells the category (education/problem awareness), and Pillar 3 sells you specifically (mechanisms/proof/differentiation).

> "pillar one builds the personal connection, pillar two crickety awareness and desire for an operator or an agency. And pillar three closes the gap between I need an operator or an agency and I need this specific operator or agency which is you or your team"

For Pillar 2A (problem naming), ask: What do coaches at your level complain about? What service-level problems do they think they have vs. actual structural issues? What have they tried that didn't work? What is the problem costing beyond money?.

> "What two coaches at this level complain about when they think no one is listening. What is the service level problem they think they have versus the actual structural issues. What have they already tried that didn't work? What is the problem costing them beyond money"

For Pillar 2B (solution positioning), sell the category of hiring an operator before selling yourself—use category selling to explain why an operator is better than alternatives (VAs, courses, coaching, DIY) and why revenue share is aligned.

> "it works through category selling a principle from marketing psychology where you sell the category before you sell the actual brand"

For Pillar 2C (outcome visualization), paint a vivid picture of daily life after hiring an operator by asking: What does a typical day look like? What can the coach focus on? What revenue milestones become possible? What surprises them after hiring?.

> "what does a typical day look like for a coach who has an operator running their back end. So let the coach get to focus on once the operational burden is removed what revenue milestone becomes possible"

Create a 'day in the life' video as an operator showing everything you do throughout the day—this subconsciously sells coaches because they imagine having you handle all that work without you explicitly pitching them.

> "make a day in life video of what you are doing day to day as an operator and this subconsciously sells a coach"

For Pillar 3A (mechanism/systems), share your specific frameworks, processes, and systems—ask what you do first when onboarding, what systems you install and in what order, and what one system has the most immediate impact.

> "what are the first three things I do when I onboard a new coaching client what systems do I install and in what specific order what can I teach that creates self diagnosis without giving away the full implementation"

For Pillar 3C (differentiation), answer why you over another operator by explaining what you do that others skip, what standards you refuse to compromise on, what experience is unique to working with you, and what you're willing to show that others won't.

> "what do I do in my process that other operators skip or don't know how to do? What standard do I hold that I refuse to compromise on? What would a coach experience working with me that they wouldn't get from another operator?"

### Ad Creative & Campaign Structure

In your first content cycle bin, focus only on content that will get views (high-engagement pieces); in your second content cycle bin (for warmed audiences), strategically select content like testimonials, use cases, and expertise demonstrations that align with your offer.

> "In content cycle bin one, we don't care about anything else. We just want to pick content that's going to get a view. That's it. In content cycle bin 2, it's a little more strategic."

Create story-driven content (screenshots of stories with captions), one-pagers, and carousels as three separate ad creative types, then run one ABO campaign with three assets (entrepreneurship interest, broad audience, retargeting) and 3-9 creatives per asset.

> "I would create three, nine different creatives, three creatives per asset..We have one campaign. We have three individual assets. We have nine different creatives"

Balance three types of creatives in your content strategy: educational-based (teaching new solutions), credibility-based (showing proof of concept), and value-based (showing direct returns and tracking sheets), with value-based creating highest conversion.

> "We have value-based creatives. We have credibility-based creatives And we have educational based creative educational based creatives is Educating people about a new solution that they are unaware about Credibility-based is leading with proof.. Value-based creatives are creatives that amplify a direct return"

Use one of three proven VSL format styles: talking head (you on camera), slide deck with talking head (Google doc/slides on left, you on right), or screen recording with voiceover, as all three perform equally well with no dominant format.

> "There's a few people now who do a style where they have a Google doc or some kind of slide deck on the left and then they're like the talking head on the right hand side.. Obviously, classic talking head content like how you see me here. This has also worked well.. just slides.. This combo is tried and true and also works really well"

Create three types of story ads: action-driven (commands), problem-solution-driven (value), and credibility-driven (social proof); credibility-driven (people sharing results) converts better than self-promotion.

> "Action driven ads problem solution driven ad and credibility driven ad.. credibility is amazing when other people tell you you are Invest you're actually the best instead of yourself instead of you telling your to best you know what I mean right? Works super effective."

Use story ads as an underutilized angle since few creators run them; people click on story ads, get engaged, and follow you—making it more effective than standard video ads for top-of-funnel audience building.

> "Story I do stories for two years and I think the beauty about story ads is that nobody is doing it..so it's like a new angle so people click on your story and then they see you can launch it on on on on Facebook manager"

Copy your best-performing Instagram stories by saving them as photos, then adding a CTA (Follow to Learn More) and running them as ads; identify top performers first before scaling spend.

> "Literally copying the best stories you go to Instagram to your phone. Look Instagram to your phone you go to archive. Hold it Tell me click on it click on safe photo on the bottom right. You go to Instagram again, you make a new story and Now you just add Follow on the bottom follow to learn more."

Build campaigns with two distinct campaign types: one cold campaign (with broad, interest stack, and lookalike ad sets) and one warm campaign (with warm audience stack), each containing 25-30 uniquely-messaged ads.

> "we have two campaigns. We typically have a cold campaign and we have a warm campaign. What we'll do is we'll have three total ad sets within that cold campaign specifically. We'll have broad and by the way, just for technicality, broad generally means just a restriction of age and a restriction of like a geographic area..Then we have what's called an interest stack..And then we do a look-alike stack."

Break ads into three separate structural parts (hook, body, CTA) for creation purposes, but ensure each complete ad is thematically unique around one singular reason to buy rather than reusing body/CTA.

> "Historically, what you might do is you look at your ads in three different parts, which to be fair in saying is technically still a great best practice to look at it in three separate parts for the creation part of things, but most people will keep the body and this PTA the same."

Articulate your offer from three core angles: the problems your audience has, the circumstances they find themselves in, and the outcomes they desire, to talk to the same pocket audience multiple times.

> "Number one, the problems that that individual has. Number two, the circumstances that that individual finds them self in. Circumstances are powerful. And in addition to that, we can also articulate ourselves from an outcomes perspective."

Test different creative formats (talking head videos, image ads, static reels, UGC, AI UGC) because CPM performance varies by format; the winning format differs by advertiser and audience.

> "There's never necessarily just one type of creative that's the dominating winner that's always proven to have lower CPMs compared to another type of creative. However, what we know is creative testing can make a dramatic difference."

Use proven copy variations from previous campaigns when launching new campaigns under time pressure—adapt 5 body copy variations from past high-performing funnels rather than creating new creative from scratch.

> "I threw five body copy variations. And in this case, I do want to point out we've run a similar funnel to this in the past. So I already had some good proven copy from that past campaign. And after reading it, I realized like it was still technically applicable to this. I just had to update it a little bit around the theme sales."

Ad structure has two critical parts: hook (first 3-5 seconds to generate attention) and body (where you layer pain points, CDAs, and solutions for conversion). Without the hook, conversion never happens.

> "When you got them over the first five seconds, they'll keep watching. And the body, we can really utilize more pain points. We can create CDAs. We can do any solution. And that's where the conversion happens."

Build video ad content with this formula: Hook (triggers curiosity) → Body (addresses pain points and solutions) → CTA (calls to action) → Credibility (proof/testimonials) → Final CTA.

> "Hook is all about curiosity. The body is all about pain points and solutions. The CTA is all about actions. Credibility is all about proof."

### Email & Messaging Strategy

Send value-dense emails (3-6 per day instead of reminder emails) that contain full video transcripts or detailed messaging rather than links, since open rates are high (50-70%) but click-through rates are low, meaning consumption happens in the inbox.

> "Rather than writing a short email and having a link click to that video, we take that whole video and we write it into a long value oriented email instead. Because click-through rates are low, open rates are high, that means the consumption's happening in the inbox itself."

Send value-dense email sequences at high frequency (starting at 3 per day, scaling to 6 if open rates don't decline); focus on providing actual value in the email body itself rather than linking out to content.

> "The consumption occurs in the email itself. So whatever you are gonna have them click through to go consume, I would have rather you just put it straight into the email itself."

Transform video content into value-dense emails by transcribing videos and rewriting them as long-form email copy using AI LLMs instead of relying on link click-through (which typically sees single-digit rates).

> "break it down into a fucking email. You know how easy this is to do with AI nowadays? You can have somebody who just mans these AI LLMs that are available to you. Throw your video in there, transcribe it, send a bunch of emails that you've already written that are in your communication style and say, Hey, turn this into a fucking email for me."

Write OTO copy as a complement to the original purchase: '
