## Sales Call & Qualifier

### Pre-Call Qualification & Lead Filtering

Use the interest spectrum to segment incoming leads by engagement level: curious (low show rate), general interest, high interest, and convicted/sold—focus acquisition on general interest and high interest prospects as they're more likely to show and close.

> The interest spectrum is very simple and starts off with curious. You then move up to general interest. From there, you have a high level of interest and from there, you have convicted aka sold.

Implement strict qualification before booking sales calls; only schedule calls with high-intent prospects who have consumed enough credibility content and understand their problems. Verify financial claims on calls before allowing low-ticket or free tier access by requiring proof of income statements or net worth documentation to prevent unqualified leads from poisoning pixel data.

> Our salespeople will literally have them show us. We'll be like, show us, improve us that you make whatever it is the amount of money is that you claim you make. Because again, it's a qualify. That's what forces that individual to get into the group.

Ask structured qualification questions that surface buying intent: "Are you currently starting or scaling your info offer?" → "Is the main bottleneck volume or quality of leads?" → "How long has this problem been a problem?" to identify true buying capacity early.

> Are you currently starting or scaling your info offer? Is the main bottleneck volume or quality of the leads? Has this problem been a problem for like three months or a year plus?

For DM-based qualification, use adaptive scripts that adjust tone based on prospect responses rather than rigid templates—give ego checks to minimal responders but match excitement with engaged prospects.

> We call it an adaptive script. So like based on where a conversation flows, we adapt. So if somebody gives us two word responses, we'll give them a massive ego check. Whereas if somebody's like very open, we'll be like more excited.

Use a pre-call onboarding questionnaire asking: what made you join, which content convinced you, what could I have done better, and what objections/fears did you have—extract this data to inform future content and sales messaging.

> You have to send them over a pre-call onboarding questionnaire.. you want to extract as much information as possible from your actual audience.. you have 10 people all told you what you posted that made them want to pay.

### DM Setting & Conversation Framework

Structure DM conversations with: intro, current situation, desired outcome, specific financial target, then indirectly qualify by asking "How far are you right now with your job compared to that X per month"—this reveals qualification early without sounding salesy.

> right now with your current job how far are you from X Y Z per month guys this indirectly right away in the first three questions indirectly tells me if they're qualified

Open DM conversations with curiosity-based language ("Yo, just curious") rather than direct selling to generate 60%+ positive response rates and create genuine engagement.

> You create a hook based on a pain point. What do I do to create curiosity? I say, yo. Just one more. Why is this guy working right?

Use this universal text conversation structure: acknowledge their statement ("Got it" or "Awesome") → answer their question directly → ask a follow-up question to continue momentum.

> I always start with like, got it. Or awesome. Or thanks for clarifying. Then you read their question, you give an answer to their question. And then you do a follow up question. This is literally every conversation.

Ask qualifying questions that isolate their specific problem: "What's holding them back from making X per month" when prospects are already in the industry to uncover the core pain point that becomes your selling angle.

> what's holding them back or what's keeping them from making x, y and z per month

Build buying intent by asking "Is that also the reason why you reached out to me because you were looking for help?" which frames the upcoming sales call as paid and positions the prospect in a buying state.

> Is that also the reason of why you reached out to me because you were looking for help, I assume. When you even ask this question, it instantly amplifies that they need help.

For colder leads from reels/posts who push back during DM setting, don't book the call immediately; instead send the problem-solving video to do 80% of the work, then use the setting script for the final 20% conversion.

> once you get to the obstacle question if dry instead of book the call what do you do is you send the video that your YouTube that solves their problem once the video is sent to you in sms reminder

Propose the call using "Would it help if" language tied to their desired outcome so it feels like their idea, not a sales pitch.

> would it help if x y z to show you how to do x y z but most importantly get you to desire outcome

For appointment setting, ask qualifying questions revealing buying capacity early (e.g., "Are you comfortable with becoming big on IG?") and pick a call time within 5 minutes to create urgency and scarcity.

> I'm interested in your offer and I think it could be a super good fit definitely you comfortable with becoming big on i g the reason why I say this is purely psychology you are comfortable with becoming big on i g you know what that amplifies the amplifies like he is going to become big on i g are you comfortable with that picking someone in five minutes it creates massive urgency and scarcity for him to keep engaged in the conversation

Always send a relevant value asset (video) matching their stated problem before proposing the sales call, then use their follow-up question as the trigger to book the appointment.

> I'll send you an asset that will solve your exact problem. If they say yes, I say the link to the asset. After the link, I say, okay, you had the time check it out. Then normally they always come back with this question and then you have them on a call.

Pass detailed notes to your closer including the prospect's fears, current situation, desired outcome, and specific obstacles so they can reference and address these on the actual sales call.

### Sales Call Structure & Closing Techniques

Structure the conversation flow as: initial conversation with opener → send 0-3 value assets depending on conversation volume → set appointment → send deliverables document before sales call → conduct sales call 3 days later.

> We have a conversation with an opener. Then after the conversation, you have a sales call where the appointment is basically set. Set appointment and then the official sales call is like three days later.

Answer all questions, objections, and expectations that would typically arise on a sales call before the call itself occurs to make the actual conversation an effortless closing event.

> Answer the questions that would otherwise occur on the sales call before the sales call occurs. It is the easiest way, the most effortless way to get somebody to show up, be cash in hand, ready to fucking give you money.

Lead sales calls through strong conviction statements and authority—tell prospects what the next steps are rather than asking; people respect leaders and pay those further ahead.

> the biggest thing that people like and the one of the reasons why I think I'm successful is because people like massive leadership when you take control over the conversation or you take control over the sales school people like people that lead because the only intention of why people are ever going to pay you is from an authority perspective

Frame objections as "questions they want to go over" rather than objections; this reframes the sales call as confirmation of fit rather than persuasion, making closes easier.

> I want to even frame it as objections I want to frame it in Having a couple of questions They want to go over To confirm what they already Things that

On sales calls, identify the #1 problem you will solve, understand their current situation (hard outcome), and frame the call around solving that specific problem.

> if we have to understand what is the number one problem that you're going to solve. And you have to frame the call as them solving the problem

Distinguish between stall objections (surface-level excuses) and legitimate reasons for delay; if it's a stall after multiple calls, use a soft close that puts accountability on the prospect.

> If you have a stall delay, you have a stall objection. It's not a substantiated reason that logically makes sense. It's an excuse. But if they have a legitimate reason, they need some time, then you don't want to blow them out.

Type 2 buyers need extended conversations (longer sales cycles) and will ask detailed questions about process, mechanics, and value retention; answer these directly rather than dismissing them as unqualified.
