# How to Reach Out and Close

You have the list. This is how you turn it into paid websites. Read this once,
then keep it open while you work the rows.

## The one idea behind every pitch

You are not selling a website. You are selling more customers, more phone calls,
more booked jobs. The website is just how they get it. Local owners do not care
about design, they care about being busy. Lead with the result, never the tech.

## Match the pitch to the lead

Look at the `website_status` and `why_reach_out` columns. They tell you which
angle to use.

| website_status | What it means | Your angle |
|---|---|---|
| NONE | No website at all | They are invisible on Google. Sell a simple site so they stop losing jobs to competitors who show up. |
| OUTDATED | Old or insecure site | Name the exact flaw from `why_reach_out` (not mobile-friendly, no padlock, says 2015) and what it is costing them. |
| BROKEN | Site would not load | Open it yourself first to confirm. Then pitch an easy rebuild. A business with reviews and a dead site is bleeding money. |
| modern + low rating | Decent site, weak reputation | Softer. Pitch a refresh plus help getting more reviews. |

The hottest lead in your whole list is a business with **lots of reviews and no
website (or a dead one)**. Real demand, no web presence. Call them first.

## Calling (your main channel for NONE and BROKEN leads)

Most no-website businesses have a phone number and no email. So you call. That is
a good thing, a call closes faster than an email.

Best times: Tuesday to Thursday, 10 to 11am or 2 to 4pm. Avoid Monday mornings and
Friday afternoons.

Every call has exactly two winning outcomes, in this order:

1. **Book a 20 minute call while you are still on the phone.** Get a day and time
   agreed before you hang up.
2. **Get the owner's personal email** so you can send the video walkthrough
   tonight, then ring back in a couple of days.

Anything else (send info to the office inbox, "call back sometime") is a polite
no. Always push for one of the two.

These same three scripts appear in your CRM while you dial, filled in for each
lead, and you can rewrite them any time in the **Scripts tab of your Google
Sheet**; the CRM uses your wording straight away.

### Phone script: no website

> "Hi, is this [owner]? My name is [you], I will be quick. I was looking for
> [trade] in [town] on Google and found you through your reviews, but I could not
> find a website for you. Is that right?"

> (let them answer)

> "You have got [X] reviews and a great reputation, but when people search
> '[trade] near me', the businesses with a website show up first and take those
> jobs. I build websites for local [trade] businesses, and I have already put a
> demo together for [business] so you can see exactly what I mean."

> "The easiest way is to grab 20 minutes with me this week and I will walk you
> through it on screen. Have you got your calendar there, what day suits you?"

> (if they hesitate)

> "No problem at all. What is the best personal email for you? I will send the
> video over tonight so you can watch it when it suits, and I will give you a
> ring back in a couple of days."

The personal email matters: info@ inboxes are where videos go to die. You want
the address the owner actually reads.

### Phone script: outdated or broken website

> "Hi, is this [owner]? My name is [you], I will be quick. I found [business] on
> Google, great reviews. I had a look at your website and [the exact flaw: it did
> not load on my phone / it still says 2015 at the bottom / it would not load at
> all]. Did you know it was doing that?"

> (let them answer, most will not know)

> "That is really common, and it quietly costs you jobs because most people check
> you out on their phone before they ring anyone. I fix exactly this for local
> [trade] businesses, and I have already put a short video together showing what
> [business] could look like."

> "The easiest way is to grab 20 minutes with me this week and I will show you
> properly on screen. What day works for you?"

> (if they hesitate)

> "Totally fine. What is the best personal email for you? I will send the video
> across tonight, and I will check back with you in a couple of days."

Naming the specific flaw is what makes them take you seriously. It proves you
actually looked.

### Voicemail (keep it under 15 seconds)

> "Hi, it's [you]. I was looking at [business] online and spotted something on
> your website that is probably costing you jobs. I have put a short video
> together showing the fix. Call or text me back on this number and I will send
> it over. I will try you again in a couple of days either way. Thanks."

Leave a reason to call back, not a pitch.

### Getting past the gatekeeper

If a receptionist or staff member answers, be warm and direct:

> "Hi, I am trying to reach the owner about the website, is that [owner name]? Is
> there a good time to catch them?"

Do not pitch the gatekeeper. Just find out who and when.

## Emailing (for OUTDATED and modern leads where you have an email)

Short. One idea. One ask. No "I hope this email finds you well." No paragraph
about yourself. Owners skim on their phone.

### Email: outdated site

> **Subject:** your website on mobile
>
> Hi [owner],
>
> Found [business] on Google, you have got a strong reputation. I had a look at
> your website and [the exact flaw, e.g. it is not mobile-friendly, so it is hard
> to use on a phone]. Most of your customers are checking you out on their phone
> first, so that is likely costing you a few jobs a month.
>
> I build and fix websites for local [trade] businesses. Want me to send a quick
> before-and-after of what yours could look like?
>
> [Your name]
> [Phone]

### Subject lines that get opened

- your website on mobile
- quick note about [business]'s site
- found you on Google
- this is probably costing you jobs

Lowercase, specific, no hype. It should look like a real person, not a campaign.

### Follow-ups (this is where most sales actually come from)

Most owners reply on the second, third, or fourth touch, not the first. If you
quit after one, you are leaving the money behind.

- **Follow-up 1 (day 2):** reply to your own email. "Hi [owner], bumping this up
  in case it got buried. Worth a quick look?"
- **Follow-up 2 (day 5):** send value. "Made a 60 second video showing the three
  things I would fix on your site, want me to send it?"
- **Follow-up 3 (day 9, the break-up):** "I will stop here so I am not a pest. If
  getting more customers from your website is ever a priority, I am one reply
  away. All the best."

The break-up email gets more replies than any other. People do not want to miss
out.

## Walking in (use your local advantage)

You are local. For the best HOT leads, drive over. Pick a quiet time (mid
afternoon, not the lunch rush).

> "Hi, are you the owner? I will be 30 seconds. I help local [trade] businesses
> with their websites, and I noticed yours [does not exist / is hard to use on a
> phone]. I am not here to sell you anything today, I just wanted to put a face
> to it and leave my number. If it is ever something you want sorted, here is my
> card."

Low pressure. You are planting a seed and standing out by showing up in person.

## Handling the common objections

- **"How much is it?"** "It depends on what you need, that is exactly what the
  quick call is for. Most of my local clients are surprised it is less than they
  thought. Can I show you some options?"
- **"My nephew / a mate is doing it."** "Nice, how is that going? A lot of those
  end up half-finished. If it ever stalls, I do this full time and turn them
  around fast. Want me to keep your details?"
- **"I'm not interested."** "No worries. Out of interest, is it because the
  website side is just not a priority right now, or you have had a bad experience
  before?" (you learn the real objection, and sometimes reopen the door)
- **"Just send me some info."** "Happy to. So I send the right thing and not a
  generic brochure, are you more bothered about getting found on Google, or
  getting more bookings from the site?" (turns a brush-off into a real answer)
- **"I'm too busy right now."** "Totally get it, that is the whole point of a
  website, it works while you are on the tools. I will keep it to 10 minutes. How
  is Thursday?"

## The numbers, so you know it is working

Outreach is a numbers game and activity is the only part you control. Rough shape:

- 100 dials -> about 20 to 30 real conversations
- 20 to 30 conversations -> about 5 to 10 genuinely interested
- 5 to 10 interested -> about 2 to 4 booked meetings
- 2 to 4 meetings -> about 1 to 2 sales

So roughly every 100 calls is a sale or two when you are starting out, and it
climbs fast as your pitch sharpens. Do not judge it on the first 20. Work volume.

## Your first session, do this today

1. Open your tracker. The list is already sorted, HOT at the top.
2. Take the top 10 HOT rows (most reviews first).
3. For NONE and BROKEN: call them. For OUTDATED: open the site, confirm the flaw,
   then call or email using that flaw.
4. Log every single touch in the `status`, `contacted_on`, and `notes` columns.
5. Aim to book the call, not close the deal. Sell the next step.

Then do it again tomorrow. The students who win are the ones who work the list
every day, not the ones with the perfect script.
