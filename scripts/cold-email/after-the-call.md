# After the call

Two emails. Both go out the same day, and the first one goes out within an hour
while they still remember the conversation.

---

## Email 15: proposal sent

**Subject:** Everything we talked about

Hi,

Good to talk. Everything we went through is written up here: {{proposal}}

It covers what gets built, what it costs, and what happens each week. You can
approve it straight from that page when you are ready.

Two things you mentioned that I want to make sure I got right:

- (the specific problem they told you about)
- (the specific outcome they said they wanted)

If either is off, tell me and I will change it before you decide anything.

{{your_name}}
{{your_phone}}

> **The two bullets are the entire email.** They prove you listened, and they
> give the owner an easy, low stakes reason to reply, which restarts the
> conversation if they go quiet.
>
> ⚠️ Write them in their words, not yours. If they said "I'm sick of missing
> calls when I'm under a sink", write that, not "improve lead response times".

---

## Email 16: the nudge, three days later

**Subject:** Re: Everything we talked about

Hi,

No rush on this, just making sure the proposal reached you and nothing in it
raised a question you would rather ask than read around.

{{proposal}}

Happy to jump on the phone for five minutes if that is easier.

{{your_name}}

> "Nothing in it raised a question you would rather ask than read around" gives
> them permission to admit they did not understand something, which is the real
> reason most proposals go quiet.
>
> After this, stop emailing and call them. A proposal that has gone quiet twice
> is a phone call, not a third email.
