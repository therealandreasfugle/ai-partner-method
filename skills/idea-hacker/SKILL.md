# Idea Hacker

Most creators burn out because they treat every post as a brand-new idea. The people who post consistently do the opposite: they take **one** strong idea and express it five different ways. This skill does that. One idea in, a week of content out, structured as a story first so it actually connects, then shaped to fit each platform.

The order matters: **find the story before you write the formats.** If you skip straight to "write me a carousel," you get generic content. If you find the human story inside the idea first, every format inherits that and the whole week feels like one clear message.

## Step 1 — Ask for the one big idea and the goal

Start simple. You only need two things to begin:

1. **The one big idea** — a lesson, story, opinion, result, or topic they want to get content out of. A brain-dump, a voice-note rant, a transcript, or even one sentence all work. If they already gave it, use it; don't make them repeat themselves.
2. **What they want to do with it** — the goal behind the content. (Examples: "get more coaching clients," "grow my email list," "build authority in my niche," "promote my workshop.") This decides what each format drives toward, especially the CTAs.

Then you produce all five formats automatically: an **email**, a **YouTube outline**, an **Instagram story sequence**, a **carousel**, and a **reel** — all in the user's own voice.

If the idea is thin or vague (a one-liner like "consistency matters"), interview them briefly first. Ask 3 to 5 of these, conversationally, not as a form:

- What happened that made you want to say this? (the moment, the story)
- Who is this for, and what are they struggling with right now?
- What did you used to believe, and what changed?
- What is the one thing you want them to walk away believing or doing?
- Do you have a number, a result, or a specific example that proves it?

Stop interviewing as soon as you have enough to find the story. You usually do after two or three answers.

## Step 2 — Find the story spine

Before writing anything, lock these down (write them out, it only takes a few lines). This is the spine every format hangs on:

- **Core message:** the one sentence the whole week argues for. Not the topic, the *take*.
- **Who it's for:** the specific person, and the pain or desire they have today.
- **The tension:** what most people get wrong, or the struggle the user lived through.
- **The turn:** the realization, the shift, the thing that changed.
- **The proof:** a number, a story, a client result, a before/after. Concrete beats clever.
- **The takeaway:** what they should believe or do after consuming it.

If any of these is missing and it matters, ask one more question. Otherwise infer it and move on.

## Step 3 — Write the five formats

Write all five. Each one is native to its platform, not the same text pasted five times. Keep the user's voice throughout (see Voice below). No emojis unless the user's voice uses them. Lead with the story and the specific, not with throat-clearing.

Output them under clear headings in this order.

### 1. Email
Conversational, one idea, written like a message to one person. Structure:
- **Subject line:** 3 options. Curiosity or a specific outcome, not clickbait.
- **Body:** open with the story or a sharp line, walk through the tension and the turn, deliver the takeaway, end with one clear next step (reply, read, watch, book). 150 to 300 words.

### 2. YouTube video
An outline a creator can film from, not a full word-for-word script (except the hook).
- **Title:** 2 options, search-friendly, pairs a specific outcome with the topic.
- **Hook (word for word, ~15 seconds):** confirm the click and call out the viewer in the first two sentences.
- **Outline:** 4 to 7 beats with one line each. Mark where the proof/story goes.
- **CTA:** one line.

### 3. Instagram story sequence
A 4 to 6 frame sequence using a simple, proven arc. One idea per frame, one line of on-screen text per frame, plus a note on the visual or interaction (poll, question sticker, DM CTA).
- Frame 1: the hook or the relatable moment
- Frame 2: the feeling or the problem
- Frame 3: the why behind it / the turn
- Frame 4: the lesson or belief
- Final frame: one CTA (reply, DM a keyword, tap the link)

### 4. Carousel
An outline for a 6 to 10 slide carousel.
- **Cover hook:** the scroll-stopper. Specific noun + number, contrarian setup, or a clear promise.
- **Slides:** one idea per slide, a short headline + one or two lines each. Build the argument: problem, then the turn, then the steps or proof.
- **Final slide:** one CTA (follow, save, or comment a keyword).

### 5. Reel
A short-form script, 20 to 45 seconds.
- **Hook (word for word):** the first line stops the scroll. If the first 2 seconds don't grab the right person, nothing else matters.
- **Body:** 3 to 5 spoken beats, conversational, one point each.
- **CTA:** one line, spoken or on-screen.

## Voice — make it sound like them, not like AI

Generic AI voice kills this. Before writing, get the user's voice:

1. **If a voice or brand file exists, use it.** Check the project for a file like `brand.md`, `voice.md`, `BRAND.md`, or a `brand/` folder and read it. If the user has a personal "write in my voice" skill, defer to it.
2. **If not, capture it fast.** Ask the user to paste 1 to 2 things they've already written (a caption, an email, a text), or to name 2 to 3 voice rules (for example: short sentences, no corporate words, British spelling, swears sometimes, uses "&" not "and"). Mirror it.
3. **Defaults that keep it human:** contractions, concrete specifics over adjectives, short sentences, no hype words ("unlock," "elevate," "game-changer," "level up"), no emoji unless their voice uses them. Bold the key number or punchline, not whole sentences.

## Output format

Deliver all five formats in one response, under the headings above, ready to copy. Put the story spine at the very top in a short block so the user can see the through-line. Keep it clean and scannable. If the user only wanted some of the formats, give them those, but offer the rest.

## One worked mini-example (for calibration, not to copy)

**Idea (brain-dump):** "I almost quit at 6 months. No clients. Then one reel about my actual process got me 3 calls in a week. The lesson: show the work, don't just talk about results."

**Story spine:**
- Core message: People don't trust claims, they trust process. Show how you actually work.
- Who it's for: a service provider posting for months with nothing to show for it.
- Tension: they post polished "results" content and it converts no one.
- The turn: one behind-the-scenes "here's how I actually do it" reel changed everything.
- Proof: 3 calls in a week from one reel, at month 6.
- Takeaway: show the work.

From that spine, every format writes itself: the email tells the near-quit story, the reel hook is "I almost quit my business at 6 months," the carousel is "Stop posting results. Post your process. Here's why," and so on. Always start here.
