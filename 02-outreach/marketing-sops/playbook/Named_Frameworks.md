### Buyer Psychology & Segmentation

The Buyer Spectrum categorizes customers into four risk-tolerance types: Type 1 (first movers, high risk, minimal proof needed), Type 2 (follow first movers, medium-high risk, some proof required), Type 3 (group thinkers, low risk, massive social proof needed), and Type 4 (cynics, lowest risk/income, validate failure). Market sophistication increases across this spectrum, requiring messaging shifts from simple claims to unique mechanisms and detailed process explanations.

> I believe there's four types of buyers.

>
The Interest Spectrum has four levels—curious, general interest, highly interested, and convicted—where deals actually close at the convicted and highly interested tiers. Map all prospects on this spectrum and work to raise them toward conviction before they engage with your offer.

> There's four levels of interest. There's curious, there's general, there's highly interested and then there's convicted. And typically convicted, anything above here, this is where deals get done.

### Revenue Engineering & Math

Implement "Million Dollar a Month Math": divide your $1M target by average order value (AOV) based on 30-day cash collected to determine the number of customers needed, then work backwards to calculate required calls, show rates, close rates, and ad spend.

> You take a million dollars, okay, and you're going to divide it by what your average AOV is currently. So whatever you actually cash collect, I'm not one of those guys that's ever going to tell you to optimize around your future revenue or your future ROI that you may or may not get from payment plans that you're putting people on. I prefer to optimize around 30 day cash collected.

>
Use "Closer Math" to anticipate hiring needs: calculate cost per call, determine how many calls each closer handles daily, then reverse engineer how many closers you need based on ad spend increases.

> If I'm spending $1,000 a day and I have a cost per call of $100, every $1,000 I spend, I get an extra 10 calls.. once I get to the point where I have a closer that's at.. seven or eight calls.. I need more closers.

>
Use "Collected Dollar Per Book Call" as your primary keystone metric—it combines lead flow, close rate, average sales price, and collection rate into one number that reveals all performance truth.

> Collected dollar per book call is a keystone metric. It takes all of that into account.

>
Organize lifetime value strategy into two timeframes: short-term (sub-30 days) and long-term (30+ days), where short-term allows you to recoup customer acquisition costs within a single credit card billing cycle.

> Short-term ideally means anything that is sub 30 days.
