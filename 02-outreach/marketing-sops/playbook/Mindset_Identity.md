## Mindset & Identity

### Identity & Self-Concept

Adopt a deliberate identity shift by intentionally choosing characteristics and beliefs of high earners rather than staying attached to limiting self-concepts; the wrong people believe they 'are who they are' while the right people actively reprogram themselves.

> "you can choose every single fucking characteristic that you currently think is your identity and every single one of them that's not beneficial to your growth you can strip away"

Change your self-image by portraying the exact person you want to become—down to their deepest characteristics, personality traits, and how they walk in a room—then place daily thoughts, habits, and actions in place to act as if you already are that person.

> "I had to portray the exact person I wanted to become and I mean to its deepest fundamentals and characteristics What kind of person I wanted to become how do they walk in the room? How do they present themselves? How were they as a person? What were their personality traits and once I understood to its finest detail What kind of person I wanted to become I then had to put actions and daily thoughts and habits into place for me to then act as if I was already that person"

Repeat small behaviors aligned with your new identity every day (how you handle money, talk to clients, dress, walk)—your nervous system learns through repetition and will eventually accept the new identity as normal.

> "Once you do this, you have to repeat small behaviors that align with that identity every second of every single day. Whether it's how you handle money, how you talk to clients, how you walk down the street, or even how you dress for the day. Your nervous system learns through repetition."

When shifting identities, expect your nervous system to trigger discomfort and fight-or-flight responses as it tries to keep you in the safe, familiar identity; winning this internal battle is critical to locking in the new identity.

> "When you try and shift your identity, your nervous system is going to try to stop you and keep you stuck in your current identity because that's where it feels most safe. As soon as you try and adapt a new identity that is bigger than your current state of self, your nervous system gets into a fight or flight mode."

Your nervous system must be in a regulated state (not in survival/fight-or-flight mode) for identity shifts to feel natural and believable; calm, regulated nervous systems are also more creative and attractive to clients.

> "if you are trying to shift your identity into someone that makes more money, but your nervous system is stuck in that survival mode, your brain will reject that new identity as unsafe or unbelievable"

Identity shift is the fastest lever for growth—spend daily convincing yourself you've already achieved the goal (e.g., '$100k month'), walk and talk as that version, and your actions and decisions will align with that identity.

> "Every day Convincing ourselves that we made a hundred k a month"

Adopt a target income identity and live as if you already make that amount, even before you actually do; your business will catch up to match your new identity through aligned decisions.

> "I made a decision right there and then that I made $30,000 a month even though I actually didn't in reality. When people ask me, yo how much do you make per month? I would tell them $30,000 a month even though I didn't."

Step 1 of identity shifting: regulate your nervous system first through deep breathing and calming techniques to signal safety to your brain before attempting to embody the new identity.

> "Step one is regulating first. Before you step into your new, more successful identity, you need to calm your nervous system first."

Belief and perception of yourself directly determines the quality of work you do and speed of execution—poor self-perception leads to 12-16 hour days of unproductive work, while confident identity leads to high-leverage actions.

> "If you have a bad perception on yourself.. The work that you do is going to be based off of your perception of yourself"

As you scale and get richer, your identity must evolve to match the new opportunities available—clinging to the old identity that got you rich will anchor you and prevent next-level growth.

> "As you get richer, your identity has to change. The old identity that got me as rich as I am now.. But the new identity, that's what's going to get you to where you go next."

Adopt your desired identity traits before the money comes—practice being charismatic, abundant, eloquent, courageous, and generous when you have nothing to attract the life you want.

> "I started being charismatic when I had nothing to my name. I used to think I was so rich internally. I trained my subconscious mind to believe that I was already rich before the money came and that's how I attracted this life."

Million-dollar earners practice the art of detachment from habits, beliefs, perspectives, and identities that no longer serve them, understanding that reaching the next level requires a new version of yourself.

> "they practice the art of detachment. There's not a lot of willingness to actually hold on to something that might not be benefiting them anymore, no matter what that thing is."

### Beliefs & Mental Models

Your brain only perceives what you're looking for—if you don't have a worldview that you're capable of earning more, you'll miss opportunities that would otherwise appear; beliefs literally determine which data points register in your consciousness.

> "you only see what you're trying to see..you reinforce whatever beliefs you have"

The narrative and beliefs held by team members about business potential often become self-limiting—sometimes the offer, marketing, and stats are fine but a false collective belief about potential limits revenue without any actual operational change needed.

> "sometimes it's just the narrative side of things which is typically spun up by people..you can conclude to a different narrative..and becomes like a collective belief that alone can limit potential"

People unconsciously manipulate their behavior to prove themselves right and validate their worldview, even when it damages results; be aware that a business owner's narrative will shape team actions in ways that make the false story come true.

> "We manipulate behavior to prove ourselves right to view the world away that we're attempting to view it."

A narrative is the unconscious story your team uses to interpret data; when this story is manipulated rather than reality-based, it prevents progress because you're optimizing decisions in a false world rather than reality.

> "Data doesn't just have a random place in our lives. Data fits in. It works its way into a specific part of a current storyline that goes on."

The longer you operate in a false narrative, the harder it becomes to accept the truth because admitting the error feels like wasting months or years; this causes people to bury themselves deeper in the lie.

> "When you're fucking so deep in the story. This fucked up narrative that to shatter the whole thing makes you feel like you wasted months or years and so you just bury your head further in the sand."

Use psychological pain avoidance as a red flag for when you're creating false narratives; if admitting the truth (like hiring incompetent people) causes discomfort, you're likely hiding from reality in a comforting lie.

> "The whole point of the book is we're trying to manipulate ourselves away from psychological pain.. Imagine that having to actively admit that the person that you've hired is a fucking dumpster fire."

Reinforce beliefs through data and stories from high achievers—seek books, case studies, and historical examples of successful people who share beliefs you want to adopt to validate those worldviews before requiring personal proof.

> "it's important to seek data until you genuinely believe it..when somebody proposes something as something for you to believe you don't just immediately inherit it unless there's something that you see"

Identify and break limiting beliefs about how much money you can make in info products; one strategic shift can increase monthly revenue 5-10x, signaling you've been underperforming due to beliefs, not capability.

> "People have limiting beliefs or on how much they can make this is no joke. I've seen it happen every single time. After an hour of pure value, they leave the call and they go and make 10 fold how much they made."

Identify and reframe limiting beliefs that prevent you from selling standalone products; their belief that people needed full courses to use AI was blocking a $324k ARR opportunity.

> "The very first step, per usual in almost anything that helps us make more money, is we got to address our mindsets. That was the key limiting belief for me. I had to address that and reframe it."

Inherited beliefs from mentors or authorities can cause you to misinterpret your own business reality and make destructive decisions; stay tapped into your actual data, not received wisdom.

> "handy down beliefs perfect little bridge for you here with that last part the illusion of what you've heard trying to be materialized in what you build can violently fuck you okay violently fuck you"

> "I think you can achieve that without trauma. I think you can achieve that without needing like the exact opposite.. it doesn't have to come from that as my point. Create non-materialistic financial reasons for higher income—family emergencies, supporting dependents, healthcare costs, and ability to help others in crisis are more emotionally binding reasons to earn aggressively than watches or cars.

> "when your family comes to you and is like hey man I need some help and it's like a serious situation..you could have earned more and you could have helped them"

> "if you don't have really strong reasons to hit million dollar months..why are you gonna give a fuck to solve the problem faster than you otherwise could"

> "if you invested 10k a month for 20 years in a row that you invest..comes out to like a little less than seven million dollars..the 4% rule..leaves him with like maybe thousands of dollars a month"

Create highly believable reasons backed by data for why you need more money—use retirement math, family obligations, and future security rather than just wanting more, as stronger personal motivations drive action.

> "you'd highly believable reasons it all starts with highly believable reasons.. you can convict yourself of whatever you'd like but the highly believable reasons that back up why you actually believe in these things is what matters most"

Tap into your deepest reasons—whether selfish, empathetic, philanthropic, or spiritual—to sustain action; the source of motivation matters less than its authenticity and power to drive consistent effort.

> "You have to find the purpose that you were lying to You have to find something Even if it's selfish Even if it's empathetically even if it's philanthropic Even if it's spiritual Whatever it is that you specifically want to start thinking through To consistently be bigger, get bigger and stay bigger."

Scale your motivation beyond material possessions (cars, houses, watches) because those motivations fade once acquired—anchor instead to a deeper mission or purpose that compounds over time.

> "Motivational drivers are dynamic, okay? Motivational drivers change this is why a lot of people they grow and then they hit some Stagnancy and contract because what motivated them to grow is no longer something that actually makes them continue onward."

Motivational drivers are dynamic and change as you progress from poor to wealthy; the things that made you want to get richer when you were poor are different from the things that'll make you want to get much richer when you're already rich.

> "Motivational drivers are dynamic. They're not constants. The things that made you want to get richer when you were poor are very different than the things that'll make you want to get much richer when you're already rich."

### Money Mindset & Scarcity

Don't let yourself become smaller than material things—paradoxically, refusing to see money as a limiting factor and aggressively acquiring assets creates a self-concept that justifies high earning; feelings of scarcity enable constraint thinking.

> "I'm not bigger than stuff..I'm not able to materialize like a fleet of cars..I'm not bigger than a house..that's a deeply unsettling mind stuff"

Rapidly ascend through checkboxes of material acquisitions (clothes, locations, cars, watches) before unlocking sophisticated thinking about money, because these things block your ability to think beyond them.

> "When you're young and you're first getting money, you've got to get to the point where you evolve to sophisticated gambler, which means you should rapidly ascend through the checkbox that are in between."

Once you've acquired the material things that once intimidated you, you evolve into the 'Sophisticated Gambler' phase where you take calculated high-probability business and investment risks instead of chasing consumption.

> "you reach a point in your submitting where you evolve into the sophisticated gambler. And as a characteristic, okay as a characteristic a million dollar month earners they typically all have this like sophisticated gambler that they start to behave through. Where what they do is they gamble with high probability bets."

The sophisticated gambler strategy: reinvest business profits to grow the business further, then take some cash profits to diversify into traditional investments and assets while maintaining business growth.

> "they rotate the cash like they get to the point where they have a bet that plays out in the business they get a fuckload of cash flies a result and then they reinvest to be able to do it all over again and they take some of that cash and they use it to get wealthy"

Frame money as a tool with keys that unlock doors for growth, investment in systems, and connection opportunities—not as something to accumulate and keep, as scarce mindset blocks expansion.

> "Imagine that. Imagine a money is just you having keys to open different locks. And every door you open provides you with even more money and even more opportunity. That's what money is. That's literally it. Money gets you a nice camera guy. Money gets you a nice videography. Nice content days."

Financial constraints limit life choices and freedom in concrete ways—more money creates optionality that directly improves life outcomes, family situations, and happiness.

> "I watched as an example.. my mom didn't have enough money to do so, you know? She was trapped because there were financial circumstances.. The point I'm trying to make is very simple. It's like that's a great example of something that would shape a belief inside of me that says, well the more money you have, the better the better things are, the more options you have, right?"

Your wallet and your brain operate independently—having the money to spend doesn't guarantee you'll emotionally endure the uncertainty, which is why most people quit before seeing results.

> "Your wallet and your brain think differently. There's a difference in how you think. When you think with your wallet versus how you think with your brain."

Build personal financial safety through consistent investments (e.g., $83,333/month in index funds); this security allows you to think strategically instead of surviving paycheck-to-paycheck, enabling bigger business bets.

> "I put a million a year in index funds. The reason I'm doing this is to build safety for later on in my life. Because I know that in the back of my head, I can always operate with a state of mind where I can strategically think about the most important decisions."

Don't convince yourself out of big goals or nice acquisitions—you must be bigger than the stuff you want, not smaller; resistance to desired purchases signals you're still intimidated by them.

> "don't underestimate convincing yourself out of big things and nice stuff and large lofty goals. Don't be smaller than the stuff okay that's a very critical thing"

You must invest excess cash flow into assets or investments so those assets generate the cash flow that sustains your lifestyle after active income stops; living purely off cash flow without investing is unsustainable.

> "You can't stop when you just have the cash flow to do what you want. You have to go in far excess of whatever that comfort level is so you can invest the excess so the excess supports after it's invested you."

The final goal is achieving a cost-of-ownership level supported by passive income; this requires you to stay focused on continuous growth and investment, not stop at the first comfortable milestone.

> "the big outcome that's so hard for most people to keep attention on is being able to sustain what you've managed to accumulate now the cost of ownership without having to work for the active cash flow anymore."

The purpose of generating high cash flow is to condense decades of normal wealth-building into years, so you can experience your goals while still young and healthy enough to enjoy them.

> "The whole point you make in a fuckload of cash is to condense the work life cycle down to a little tiny window. So you maximize the fuck out of your earning potential in a short period of time."

Recognize when you're coasting (doing non-revenue activities, making excuses, falling into comfortable routines) and immediately correct course—coasting directly reduces your cash flow.

> "You might just not know what to do, right, but seeking what to do is not that hard. It just takes time and effort to educate yourself and then turn it into action. And the speed in which you move through that process to get back into action is the speed in which you're probably going to start growing again."

Stop clutching onto small amounts of cash due to fear—take multiple small financial bets across different strategies, and some will fail but others will hit and produce outsized returns.

> "You sit here and you learn lessons like this, you see the results, you see the outcomes, and yet for some reason you still clutch for dear [ __ ] life onto that little bit of cash that you string onto, and then you just refuse to risk and bet."
