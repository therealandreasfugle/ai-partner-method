# The follow up sequence

**Most of your replies come from these, not from the first email.** Send the
opener, then these three, then stop. Four touches total, then the break up.

Timing: day 1 opener, day 3, day 7, day 14, break up day 21.

⚠️ Every follow up replies to the original thread. Same subject line with `Re:`,
never a fresh subject. A new thread reads as a new stranger. A reply reads as
somebody who is still here.

---

## Email 11: day 3, the bump

**Subject:** Re: (whatever the opener was)

Hi,

Bumping this in case it landed at a bad moment.

The site is still up here: {{site}}

{{your_name}}

> Two lines. That is the whole email. A long follow up to an unanswered email is
> the fastest way to look desperate.

---

## Email 12: day 7, add something

**Subject:** Re: (same thread)

Hi,

get a text and an email within seconds with their number, and they get an
instant reply telling them you are on it.

That gap, between somebody enquiring and somebody hearing back, is where most
local businesses lose the job to whoever answered first.

{{site}}

{{your_name}}

> **The only follow up rule that matters: add something new, never just ask
> again.** This one adds the thing they actually care about, which is not the
> website, it is not losing the job to a faster competitor.

---

## Email 13: day 14, the easy no

**Subject:** Re: (same thread)

Hi,

I will stop after this one.

If the timing is wrong, that is completely fine, just say "not now" and I will
check back in a few months rather than keep emailing you.

> Giving them a clean way to say no gets you more yeses than another pitch would.
> Roughly one in five replies here, and about half of those turn into calls.

{{site}}

{{your_name}}
{{your_phone}}

> **This is consistently one of the highest reply rates in the whole sequence.**
> Do not skip it because it feels like giving up. It is not a trick either. Mean
> it, and actually stop.
>

> do.
