## Hooks & Openers

### Research & Due Diligence Openers

Acknowledge the prospect's natural research instinct in due diligence videos by positioning yourself as their guide: "You're likely going to do research on me. Let me help you do it right now so you can decide if this is worth your time." This reframes research from a barrier into a collaborative process.

> Hey, thank you so much for booking your call. You're likely curious about who I am, who my company is..most importantly, at this phase, you're likely considering well, how bad could things actually get?..let me show you how bad things could actually get. I'm going to go right now and I'm going to help you do your research

Promise to walk prospects through three distinct research areas: your company, your product/service, and the opportunity itself. This structure gives them permission to evaluate you while keeping them on your timeline.

> I get where you're at now. You want to help understand if this is the right thing for you or not and I'm going to help you do all your research on the opportunity. I'm going to help you do the research on our product or service. I'm going to help you do the research on us.

### The First 3-5 Seconds Window

Viewers make a binary decision in the first 3-5 seconds: either they click off immediately or watch the full ad—there's no middle ground. Master this window or lose them entirely.

> The average attention span per post has fallen to 1.8 seconds per post within the news feed. Don't start an ad with things that nobody gives the fuck about.

> People either click off in the first three seconds or they watch the full ad. It's never like a duration. Let's say an ad is like 20 seconds. They'll never click off at 10 or 12. It's always they watch the full ad or they watch like three seconds.

In these first seconds, avoid generic attention-getters ("stop scrolling") and jump straight into value-dense information or a curiosity trigger.

Lead with pain points in the first 3-5 seconds to immediately demonstrate you understand the viewer's situation. Use specific, real-world problems rather than broad statements.

> You're posting content, you're posting roads, you're posting YouTube, and you're seeing reviews go up yet no cash is being collected and your setter barely books any calls.

> Focus on hooks that trigger pain points. Not saying, okay if you're a coach making 5 to 10k per month. I'm saying like, your lead flow has been shit.

Use controversial or emotionally triggering statements in the first 3 seconds rather than proof, credibility, or testimonials. Credibility comes later once attention is locked.

> The most important lever is you don't have time to show proof of concept in a three-second ad where you need to capitalize on their attention. You want to say the most controversial shit. Like the beauty about ads is it's not about testimonials and credibility in the first three seconds.

Use odd, non-round numbers (e.g., $11k instead of $10k) to trigger curiosity and make people think in the opening seconds. The moment they start thinking, attention sticks.

> if you're an agent's the making 11,000 dollars from up. The reason why it's to 11 is because it makes people think.. The moment you can get people to make them think for themselves in the first three seconds. Let's say you say okay, I run an agent's making then they're thinking okay. Why does this guy say 11k.

### Pain Point & Problem-Based Hooks

Trigger the deep-rooted pain and problems prospects are running away from to maximize attention; generic messaging won't compete for their focus.

> you have to understand what is the deep-rooted problem and the deep pain that your prospects and that your audience is trying to run away from. And then you have to, using content, you have to trigger those responses and you have to trigger those problems in your content to maximize attention as much as possible.

Angle hooks around pain points rather than directly addressing the person—this captures attention without feeling like generic targeting. The viewer doesn't feel targeted, yet you've activated their pain point.

> When you speak directly to their pain points, they don't, like, you know what's the beautiful thing about that? It's like, they don't, for them, it doesn't feel like you're speaking to them. But you're still speaking to them and you still got their attention because you trigger one of their pain points.

Use specialized vocabulary (capital, dry powder, earnings disclaimers) that only your target audience understands to naturally qualify and filter in headlines and messaging.

> Rich people use words like capital. Rich people use words like dry powder. You know what that means. But the wrong person watching this, they have no fucking clue what that means.

### Results-Based & Authority Hooks

Lead with your biggest measurable result (profit, revenue, or client outcomes) as your hook to trigger relatability and desire immediately.

> This is $75,000 in cash collected in the last five days four days.. meaning Related ability comes into place.. it either triggered you or didn't trigger you

When using results-based hooks, establish credibility by explaining the mechanism or reason why before diving deeper into value. This prevents skepticism.

> we want to into me a certain that credibility and authority offer it so they don't just assume that we're on nobody so of course you're this strategy doesn't have anything in it. This one our former strategy made us 47k in five days

Don't post lecture-style "how-to" content without authority—instead take the angle of "here's how I got results" and always lead with a big number or wow factor in your title/thumbnail.

> If you don't already have insane authority and your packaging is lecture-esque your video will most likely fall. The reason my videos do good is because I'm not trying to come up as a lecturer or a know-it-all and instead I'm taking the angle of here's how I got results

### ICP Callouts & Qualification Hooks

Use direct ICP callouts in headlines that simultaneously attract ideal clients and repel unqualified ones. This dual-purpose qualification happens before prospects even engage.

> I have a headline that calls out the ICP it is direct towards their desire which simultaneously scares way the people that we don't want in our funnel as you can see here I'm literally calling out you know growth operators stuck at three to twenty thousand dollars a month

Distinguish between dream follower hooks and ICP hooks: dream follower hooks should cast a wide net with results-based or wow-factor hooks so the algorithm pushes broadly; ICP hooks should specifically call out and disqualify others to nurture.

> The hook didn't qualify or disqualify anyone and so that means anyone in the info space, even outside the info space, is qualified to watch this video

### Hook Psychology: Making People Question Themselves

A hook's primary job is to make people question themselves, not just create attention. Frame hooks around a transformative promise tied to their existing assets or problems.

> What is a hook? A hook needs to create attention. That's the thing that a hook needs to do. Yes, obviously. But the main thing that a hook needs to do is it needs to make people question themselves.

Use direct curiosity triggers (e.g., odd numbers, mystery statements) to get people thinking in the first few seconds. The moment they start thinking, they stay watching.

> by attracting attention the first three five seconds of the reel You now box yourself that window of someone maybe your potential client looking at your video and that is the time when you focus your expertise

Open with discovery and uniqueness framing—use phrases like "unique entryway" and "elements of discovery" to make viewers curious about what comes next.

> Can't wait to show you this front yard pool and truly a yard pool of discovery and elements that are so unique and artistic.

### Webinar & Live Event Openers

Ask for chat engagement early ("comment yes if you're ready to learn") to pre-frame audience for compliance later in the sales pitch.

> comment yes if you're ready to learn. This is also kind of getting them in that state where you're pre-framing the yes

Use the pre-pitch session (30-45 minutes) to strategically frame character, identity, and ways of thinking through storytelling—it should appear as mentality content, not a sales pitch.

> prepitch is you being able to successfully frame somebody. You're trying to align the character

Retain 80% of peak webinar attendees until the pitch by offering bonuses for staying live. This ensures maximum exposure to your offer when it matters most.

> From the peak show rate to the time of the pitch itself, you want to have about 80% retention. Okay. That means 80% of the people that were there live at the peak show rates should still be there live during the time of the pitch.

### Demonstration & Format Hooks

Lead with the phone call/voice capability when promoting an AI clone or product in demonstration reels; voice interaction is more compelling for conversion than text-only chat.

> The demonstration, I used the phone call version. Surprisingly, there was a lot of active buyers coming because you could talk on the phone with them.

Use video thumbnails (via Wistio or VitaLitics) that demonstrate the specific point viewers care about. If using an image thumbnail for page speed, ensure it invites viewers to watch by showing what they'll get.

> We like Wistio, we like VitaLitics for this. Not a lot of other players that are actively being used

### Urgency & Believability

Create urgency-based breakout videos using highly believable, real-world reasons aligned with your prospect's worldview that make them feel they must act now. This approach can lift show rate by 14% or more.

> Urgency. People need a reason to actually do something. And it has to be a highly believable reason that is aligned with their worldview.

### Story Sequence & Anticipation Hooks

Use mystery hooks like "Something big is happening tomorrow" combined with a timer to create anticipation and get people to click through story sequences.

> First day on the story, something big is going back tomorrow.

Use a Q&A story format to pre-handle objections, educate audience, and build trust before sales calls. Answer questions about your mechanism/difference, results, and process so prospects arrive pre-convinced.

> what this is going to do is it's going to educate your audience which is going to make them more likely to apply. Number two, it's going to break down objections to when they do go ahead and apply

Show proof of applications in real-time (e.g., flip camera to show 1,500+ applications on screen) because this triggers FOMO and herd psychology, causing more people to apply just from seeing others apply.

> When they see that there is over 1,500 applications and they keep proof of that which he flips his camera and shows the screen where he shows all the applications, people are like, damn, everyone else is doing it. Like I don't want to miss out on this.

Use scarcity messaging ("X spots remaining") and build FOMO in final days of launch (e.g., "5 out of 20 spots left") to push fence-sitters to apply before cutoff.

> Last day, we want to let them know it's the last chance to apply and that five out of 20 spots are left

### Contrarian & Belief-Shifting Hooks

Create contrarian video hooks that contradict your own popular content to capture attention—for example, if you typically promote a funnel, make a video saying to stop using it instead.

> let's say I made a video. So this video is about how to make a ton of money with your book call funnel. But let's say my next video was top using the book of call funnel. Do this instead. That video is going to get a lot of attention

### Problem-Agitation-Solution Content Strategy

Lead with problem-agitation before solution—help your audience understand why they haven't achieved their goal yet and break their limiting beliefs before pitching your offer.

> Provide value and help them understand why they haven't achieved their goal yet. Really dig into their pain points and help them realize they need to make changes, break limiting beliefs and give them new ones. Okay. That way when you do post CTA, you've already broken their beliefs, whatever you're trying to sell, they're going to believe you, right?

### DM Opener Strategy

In DM openers, avoid open-ended questions; instead, use binary yes/no questions to make it easy for prospects to respond and allow you to lead the conversation toward booking calls.

> Don't say, what is the current bottleneck in your business? Say, fire is the current bottleneck in your business? Is it the clarity on how to start it? Or is it the lead generation?..you increase response rate from like 5% to instant 20%

Use extremely short, curiosity-driven DM openers (e.g., "yo") to warm prospects. This achieves 60% response rate from followers who see your content.

> My opener is yo Oh, oh done The reason why is because people ask themselves this guy next thing 60,000 photos of mainstream made three million dollars why does guy message me yo

### Content Intent & Motive

Every Instagram reel needs a clear motive and structure behind it—post with intent (to gain followers, build authority, get DMs, shift beliefs) rather than posting blindly.

> you need to understand why are you posting this piece of content? What is the genuine overarching goal that you want to achieve from people viewing this video

### Polarizing Question Openers

Open YouTube videos with a polarizing question (e.g., "Would you rather grind a 9-5 or start your own business?") to segment audience and get people watching who are predisposed to your offer.

> Would you rather grind a nine to five or start your own business? If you say start my own business, I chose to build my own recycling business
