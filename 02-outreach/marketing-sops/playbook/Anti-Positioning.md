## Anti-Positioning

### Authenticity & Credibility

Never fake testimonials, exaggerate income claims, or fabricate asset ownership—maintain credibility by being truthful even when it's unflattering, and avoid "larping" (flexing assets you don't own).

> Have you ever lied or exaggerated about an income number online? No. True? Hell yeah. Have you ever faked testimonials? No. True?

> Have you ever flexed something you don't own? No. True? Yeah, I hate when people do that. I think they call it larping. Yeah, larping. I do not I do not larp.

Never share your revenue numbers publicly because it only caters to skeptics and validates uncertainty; instead, let your track record and social proof speak to qualified buyers.

> I will never share those revenue numbers because that caters to the skeptics.

Don't publicly name or showcase individual client success stories because it attracts copycats and low-quality buyers; keep that information exclusive to paid community members.

> I'm never going to name those fuckers. There's never going to be a video that's like, oh, hey guys, here's like the exact fucking name of every single person we've ever helped crack a million dollars a month.

### Audience Targeting & Positioning Clarity

Talk at the highest level to attract everyone below that level; talking at the lowest level causes you to miss people above that gap.

> Generally, when you talk at the highest level, you attract everybody below that level. When you talk at the lowest level, obviously you're going to miss everybody that's above that gap.

Explicitly state income thresholds to repel low-earners and attract high-earners, such as "You shouldn't be watching this if you don't already make at least 100 grand a month."

> You shouldn't even be sitting here watching this if you don't already make at least 100 grand a month. I help rich people get richer. I don't help people start.

Explicitly state who your offer is for and who it's not for in your messaging to actively deter low-intent buyers before they enter your sales funnel.

> My content is for rich people trying to get richer. My content is for guys, ideally like you already making a couple hundred grand a month or already making a couple of mill a month.

Avoid creating content targeted at broad markets; instead, narrow down to a specific client avatar with defined age range, deep-rooted problems, and desired outcomes.

> the reason why you're stuck is because you're making content that's targeted towards a broad market. Even though it might be trading, coaching, SMMA, e-com, whatever it might be, it's too broad. You have to actually narrow down the exact client you're speaking to.

Avoid positioning as a generic coach (e.g., "fitness coach")—instead use premium positioning with extreme specificity (e.g., "ex-military men ages 28-60, lose 15 pounds and gain muscle in 12-16 weeks"), which allows premium pricing and reduces competition.

> If you're a coach, a fitness coach in general, don't portray yourself as just a fitness coach.. An example of prime and precision, premium positioning is coaching ex-military men between the ages of 28 to 60, on how to lose an extra 15 pounds of weight and gain muscle in 12 weeks or less.

The Paradox of Choice—positioning yourself broadly creates decision fatigue and anxiety for prospects; extreme niche positioning removes you from direct competition and makes you the obvious choice.

> While we think that we want more options, the opposite is in fact true. More options leads to increased sense of anxiety, indecision, decision fatigue, and dissatisfaction.

Never target ICPs directly by their profile (e.g., "if you're a business owner"); instead target pain points so people self-select based on relatability.

> you never target an ICPs profile directly meaning you never say if you're a coach and you want to do XYZ you utilize this that work in 2023 you never say if you're a business owner and you're that doesn't work in an ad anymore

Make middle and bottom funnel content micro-niche specific in titles (e.g., "call funnel" instead of just "funnel") to attract only your ideal customers rather than casting a wider net.

> I mentioned that this is for a call funnel in specific. This is only targeting people that are running a call funnel. That right there is micro-neched.

### Language & Messaging Sophistication

Use language that matches your target demographic's vocabulary—rich people say "liquidity," "capital," and "dry powder," not "cash" or "money"—or they'll perceive you as an outsider/imposter.

> Rich people have dropped the word money, cash, like dollars from their vocabulary. Like, it's don't say things like this, they don't say these words, they say capital, they say liquidity.. if I was talking to rich people and I said, how do you want to turn your cash into more money? You want to make more money? You know, it's like, yeah, they know what you're saying, but to them, it's like, you're clearly not fucking rich dude, because that's not what rich dudes say.

Your funnel pages and questions should signal you understand your target audience's language and behavior—if they reveal ignorance (like asking "did you watch the video?" or using generic vocabulary), sophisticated buyers immediately perceive you as an imposter.

> Sometimes the questions that you ask and or the messaging that's on the funnel is you exposing yourself for not actually being the person that should be selling to the types of deals and the people that are on that page.. Sometimes your dumbass questions that you ask or the things that you say on the page immediately reveal that you have no idea what the fuck you're talking about and that you're an imposter.

If you notice inconsistency in getting qualified buyers, first check if it's a messaging problem (looking/sounding/acting the part) before blaming pixels or ad targeting—most high-ticket businesses fail due to not playing the character correctly.

> If you have inconsistency in the right people coming through exclusively, it's likely a messaging problem. Could be a pixel conditioning problem and I have a great video on my page on pixel conditioning mastery that you can check out, which I think would be a great follow up to this one if you haven't seen that one yet.

Don't post the same generic reels as everyone else or sound like your competitors—differentiation in how you explain value (not just what value you offer) is what separates high-earners from the crowd.

> You don't have a personal brand if you post the same reels as everyone else and you sound the same as everyone else.

Avoid generic frameworks and basic content templates your competitors use—be intentional and unique in your messaging or you'll get commoditized in a saturated market.

> Enough of that. Be intentional with what you do. Be intentional with what you post. Otherwise you will get commoditized

Remove generic labels like "entrepreneur" from your bio because your actual work already communicates it—instead, lead with your specific achievement like "founder of [specific business]."

> Remove entrepreneur. Because yes. Because people know that your entrepreneur based on your profile. What you do, it doesn't add any value.

Avoid saying "What I could do for you is get you on a call"—instead say "Would it help if.." to frame the call as their solution to their problem, not as you offering something, which reduces no-shows and increases buy-in.

### Positioning Sales & Offers

Position your sales team as program experts who properly communicate the subject matter expert's value, rather than as the expert themselves—this prevents objections about why the expert isn't closing.

> I don't believe that closers should pretend that they are the expert. What you want to do, it's position them as program experts.

Avoid positioning your offer to low-risk buyer types; if you sell high-action offers (that require risk-taking), Type 3-4 buyers will fail to execute, creating refunds and reputation damage.

> My offers require you to take risks. They require you to put your nuts on the table here and there and take a financial bet in order to see some cash return.

Position your offer as so exclusive that ideal customers actively want to work hard for the privilege of joining; waiting lists and visible scarcity signal quality and strengthen desire.

> the best agencies are always maxed out like they have like a waiting list like when you actively want to work with one of these really good marketing agencies like they're probably gonna tell you to get fucked and just put you on a list

Exclusivity tactics don't prevent scale—they enable easier scaling by reducing operational burden while increasing per-unit revenue and customer quality.

> Stop making your entire culture around close rate. Close rate does not mean that you're the most efficient rep.

Never target by job title, employer, or school; instead target by interest and behavior (engagement, profile visits) to get qualified audiences without reaching the wrong people.

### Market Dynamics

Low barriers to entry in info space mean low-quality coaches and scammers proliferate, creating stigma—but this concentrates demand toward proven top performers, making it easier for legitimate operators to dominate if they build credibility.

> There's no bears of entry in the info space so everyone comes in. They deliver false news. They don't deliver properly and they start to create a bigger stigma. All these people that get burnt are all going to flock and go to that one person or top individuals.

Reject the narrative that the info/coaching space is dying—it's cyclical; failures of bad actors are healthy market corrections that clear the field for legitimate education companies with real outcomes.

> A bunch of fucking idiots that just do courses come into the game.. they drive it into the ground.. those people become reinspired again but they're new people now and they do it all over again.

### Business Model Dependency

Avoid single-client dependency—it creates one revenue stream risk and prevents you from acquiring new clients; constantly acquire new clients and maintain multiple revenue sources to stay resilient.

> Don't put your identity into one client

Avoid the "boyfriend model" (one client, doing everything, revenue share only) because it creates key-person risk, zero scalability, burnout, and fragile income that can collapse to zero overnight.

> I could literally go from a six-figure earner to $0 overnight, having to start all over again
