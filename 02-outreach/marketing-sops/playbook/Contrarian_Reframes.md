## Contrarian Reframes

### Email & Communication

**Frame imperfect, casual reply-based emails as a strength—authenticity outperforms polish.** Use typos, short sentences, and rapid sends as proof of genuine human communication rather than corporate marketing copy. Use sparingly with warm audiences rather than blasting cold lists at high frequency.

> it works tremendously well. Initially, we tell clients and we tell our inner circle members, hey, use this sparingly, like use it for people who haven't seen it yet, rather than just consistently blasting it to your entire list at a high frequency.

**Signal firm pricing implicitly through language and brand references—mention premium brands and acknowledge downsides rather than aggressively selling.** This approach signals confidence in your pricing without explicitly stating a no-discount policy.

> It's also been telling you like you're not going to get a discount. That's essentially what you've peppered in there, whether you realize it or not.

### Scarcity & Capacity

**Reframe capacity constraints into a value-add—present your inability to scale as proof of exclusivity and care.** Capping at a fixed number of clients makes remaining slots more valuable as they get filled, increasing pricing power.

> Instead of doing that.. The scarcity of 'okay, we're going to cap it at 250 folks. Like, that's it.' What that does is it makes the slots of the remaining 250 more valuable as they continue to get filled.

### Pricing & Monetization

**Test pricing based on objections, closing rates, and immediate yes-answers rather than following one 'correct' strategy.** If customers say yes immediately, that's a signal to raise prices. Build pricing around market response data.

> If anyone tells you in the market right now, there is one specific pricing strategy to follow or one specific pricing calculation to follow, they're bullshitting.

**Favor DM payments over booking sales calls first because prospects willing to pay without meeting you signal extreme trust and higher commitment.** A person paying $45K-$67K for coaching through DMs without a face-to-face meeting demonstrates exceptional brand authority and likelihood of program completion.

> The reason why I favor so much of my clients pay through the DMs, especially with their own clients is because there's no better form of commitment when someone sees your content pays through the DMs where they're even needing a sales call. Imagine how much brand authority you have if a person that has not even met you face to face is willing and able and wanting to pay you $45,000, $67,000 for coaching.

### Sales Funnels & Conversion

**Webinars are lower-commitment alternatives to sales calls for warm audiences, reducing friction and increasing engagement.** This approach works better when prospects haven't encountered your full story yet.

> [Webinar positioning as lower friction than sales calls]

— (from Ben Bader's 2024 Inner Circle Mastermind Talk)

**Profile funnels outperform traditional VSL funnels because prospects self-qualify by visiting your profile first.** They intentionally choose to call after seeing proof, making them higher-intent than cold leads from ads.

> the profile funnel in case is like a funnel that we've generated to be able to solve the problem of like having very very high cost at creating an efficient at creating stop creating efficiency

**Optimize internal business metrics (show-up rate, booking rate, closing rate) rather than always chasing new leads.** Small improvements to conversion (e.g., 60% to 80% show-up rate, 10% to 20% booking rate) can 4X revenue without increasing ad spend or content volume.

> The only thing that changes is you can get your show up rate from 60 to 80%, which is not a massive increase. Very realistic. You're booking rate from 10 to 20% and your closing rate.. you four X-ray business

**When prospects don't show up to calls or webinars, the real issue is insufficient pre-call information, not disinterest.** Treat no-shows as an education gap—prospects lack the critical information that would make your offer a higher priority than competing demands.

> They failed to get you a key piece of information that would have made whatever that other thing was, a lower priority compared to that webinar.. What if there was some critical piece of information that could have increased your intent dramatically?

**When sales teams claim leads are 'cold' or 'unqualified,' the real problem is usually insufficient information density before the call.** Treat it as an education problem. Leads without pre-call context become education calls, not sales calls, and salespeople blame the leads instead of fixing the backend.

> people show up to a sales call with a lack of information it's no longer a sales call it's an education call and when it's an education call your sales people typically say stupid shit like all the leads aren't qualified

**Most people fail at sales conversions not because of front-end marketing but because they neglect back-end selling.** Low show rates, long sales cycles, and low closes are symptoms of insufficient pre-call education and framing, not bad leads.

> all of those symptoms are signs that you are currently failing with back end selling

### Messaging & Positioning

**Never sell against a trend—sell with it.** When AI threatened copywriting demand, the creator who pivoted from selling against AI to teaching copywriting with AI recovered from $300K to $600K/month within 2 months and hit $800K the following month.

> never sell against a trend. I'll tell you that right now. Don't fight it.

**When the market becomes aware of alternative solutions your competitors offer, reframe messaging to explain why your original approach is better rather than defending it.** Proactive messaging beats defensive positioning (e.g., "why you need to learn to do it yourself vs. having someone else do it").

> the messaging had to update to, okay, well now that the market's aware of those options, let's start saying why it's a bad idea to have somebody do it for you and why you need to do it yourself. Bang, huge response immediately again because we just simply updated and pivoted how we were communicating what we could do.

**Focus your messaging on pain points and desired outcomes rather than on the person or lifestyle.** Pain point-driven copy converts better than profile-driven copy because audiences get tired of person-focused hooks.

> when you focus on a profile, people are going to get tired of that..So instead of using a hook to focus on for example, an individual person, you want to focus the hook on a pain point

**Use the diagnosis effect: when you accurately name a prospect's specific problem before offering a solution, they automatically assign you authority.** Similar to how a doctor who understands your symptoms earns trust before prescribing medicine.

> when someone feels deeply understood in their struggle they automatically assign authority to the person who named it. And this is called the diagnosis effect.

**Use prepitch content to reframe risk-taking and time compression as characteristics of successful people, then position your offer as a vehicle for achieving faster results.** Frame your pitch as an example of a "proper risk"—an offer presenting itself inside the event.

> you would say look the people who make the most the people who get the best results like they know when to take a proper risk and like what does a proper risk look like and then you break down essentially what you're about to pitch them on as an example where you're like a great risk can look like an offer presenting itself to you inside of an event like this

**Break limiting beliefs in stories by showing counterexamples—if prospects fear no time, show a mentee with no time who succeeded in days.** Preemptively address objections through belief-shifting story elements.

> Mark joined earlier this week and in just a few days he's already learned more from me than he did six months with his previous mentor. I'm breaking the objection they instill on themselves as the future prospect on not having the time to learn

**Reframe the objection 'I don't have time' by showing mentees who realized 'you don't have time, you make the time' after a strategy call.** This makes time a non-issue by shifting the prospect's perception of their own agency.

> he realized that you don't have the time you make the time and right now, fast forward six months later, he's making 10k per month

**Identify and reframe prospects' surface objections as three larger root problems: limited time, lack of knowledge/direction, or fear of failure.** Help them understand they're in a bigger rut than they initially thought, requiring deeper help.

> instead of that small problem, they have a big problem, one of these three, which is going to help them understand that they are in a bigger rut than they actually think they are.

**Don't position the business model as easy or get-rich-quick; instead position it as requiring real time and effort.** This attracts serious prospects and sets correct expectations rather than filtering for dreamers expecting instant results.

> we don't want people to think that doing whatever the business model is is insanely easy and they're going to get results like right away with that happen and do any work because genuinely I mean, there really isn't get rich quick schemes out there.

### Content & Audience Building

**Master indirect selling through pull marketing rather than relying on CTAs, polls, and constant outreach.** This approach generates higher-ticket closes from audiences who don't realize they're being sold to.

> the selling is done all indirectly. While many people, while many of you watching rely on CTAs, on polls, on constant outreach, I do the opposite.

**The difference between an audience and a following: an audience spectates, a following believes and acts on everything you say.** Build the latter through credibility and personality.

> there's a very big difference guys between an audience and a following an audience can be someone that just spectates you a following is someone that will migrate to whatever you say if you say a belief they'll believe it if you go towards something they'll believe it

**Don't start posting or selling immediately—first understand your prospect's current situation, problems, and deeply rooted desires before crafting any marketing.** Market research precedes marketing.

> The first lesson I'll tell you guys in marketing is don't start posting content right away. Do not start selling right away. You have to understand a couple things that matter so much.

**Avoid TikTok for coaching/info offers targeting 19-27 year old men because the audience has 'brain rot,' low attention span, and seeks quick dopamine hits rather than commitment.** Use Instagram and YouTube instead for serious, qualified leads.

> The main reason why I wouldn't use TikTok is due to a highly-enqualified audience which guys I'll be honest with you, a lot of people that are on TikTok have brain rot, low attention stand, low quality. They're just in there for a quick fix, quick dopamine hit, and then they're out.

**Story sequences are underutilized compared to reels and YouTube because they're more real, customizable daily, and less commoditized.** Every story can be different and personalized, making them more valuable than standardized reels.

> it's an untapped angle of marketing Right, it's like something that nobody does because people do views Reels and people do youtube videos.. the story It's custom it's just custom story so it's every single day is different

**Reels generate attention but Stories generate conversion.** Post consistently on Stories (not just Reels) because they create deeper personal connection and lower the friction to DM. Together they're powerful; separate, they're not nearly as effective.

> Reels equal attention and Stories equal conversion, which is why together, they are such a power duo, but separate, they are not nearly as powerful

**Stop focusing on viral views as YouTube success metric; instead, create a 'binge-worthy channel' by cross-linking related videos so viewers spend 7+ hours total and become buyers.** If you're not the source of information, viewers will get stuck in someone else's funnel and you lose the lead.

> if you are not the source of information and you're leaving some information out on the table they're going to go to someone else find their channel get stuck in their binge worthy channel and their binge worthy channel and you then lose that lead

**Long-form YouTube (15-60+ minutes) outperforms short-form because you can't change minds, build emotional depth, or break limiting beliefs in 15-120 seconds.** Use YouTube to tell your full story and mechanism thoroughly, as the trust-building middle of your funnel bridge.

> with short form 15 to 60 seconds or even 120 seconds is not enough time to actually change someone's mind you can't build a deep emotional you can't sell your unique mechanism you can't break limiting beliefs thoroughly

### Customer Experience & Delivery

**Client experience matters more than perfect delivery—many coaches earning $300K-500K/month have mediocre course content but excellent client experience.** Focus on experience because it drives referrals and retention.

> People don't care only about the outcome they care about the experience So I know so many clients one of the other clients are making three to five hundred thousand dollars a month and to be very honest they're delivery Isn't great

### Beliefs & Decision-Making

**Avoid making conclusive determinations like 'my product is dead' based on incomplete testing.** Better solutions exist if you have updated data and the right people executing. Most failures are information gaps, not product failures.

> There are infinitely better. Faster, more short term, probable to work solutions. If you just had updated data, if you just had the right data to use.

**Inherited beliefs from people you admire can drive harmful behavior even without data supporting them.** A client running a $2-3M/month business who believed his product would die in one year actually sabotaged it through distracted behavior, not market forces.

> This was a handy down belief. This was a belief that came not even from running previous businesses that the guy had. This was a belief that came from somebody that the guy admired.

**Avoid jumping to conclusions about tools or agencies not working after minimal testing.** Failing ads or agencies often reflect poor execution and account structure, not the tool's viability. Check if best practices were actually followed.

> When you look at the account structure and it's fucked like it looks like none of the best practices that should be there.. They disrespect the machine learning best practices.

**Be extremely careful about the conclusions you draw from single events or limited data points.** Avoid false causal conclusions (like "Brussels sprouts give me headaches" when you simply didn't drink water) that become embedded narratives.

> It's like when you're a kid and you had Brussels sprouts for the first time and then you didn't drink any water the rest of the day and you ended up having a headache and you're like, oh, Brussels sprouts give me a headache.

### Advertising & Market Dynamics

**The fact that 22-29 out of 30 ads receive minimal distribution is not a failure—it's a requirement of Andromeda's design.** These ads are necessary for the algorithm to identify and surface the 1-3 winners effectively. High-volume, well-diversified creative is required for the algorithm to work best.

> Here's the good news. From what we found, it's actually really good things that this occurs and I'll explain why. First of all, the ones that don't get reached, they aren't bad, okay? Don't demonize them..Andromeda requires you to have a high volume of well-diversified creative for it to do its job the best.

**Use agentic AI tools to research third-party data from credible sources and institutions, then create urgency-based video scripts based on market trends, not company hype.** Create urgency based on validated external data, not internal narrative.

> go out there and do real-world research and I want you to find me legitimate reasons that would create urgency in an individual who's considering this type of real estate investment and why they have to act on it now. Don't consider the company at all. Consider third-party data that can be validated by sources credited to high-authority institutions

**Moving from organic to paid ads is not harder work—it's a different game with higher daily stakes but similar or less effort than organic hustle.** Reframe this transition as a mindset shift, not a difficulty increase.

> instead of just putting all your eggs in your organic basket and just hoping and creating everything goes the right way You buy back a lot of control.. It's not necessarily harder. It's just different

**Most small-ticket purchases come from 'in-market' buyers; true scale (30-33% of market) comes from convincing 'need-convinced' and 'mass market' demographics who don't yet know they want your product.** This is where real cold paid advertising scale comes from.

> The scale occurs when you crack these other two layers which are anywhere from like 30 to 33 percent..the Need Convinced Demographic..This is where the real money is.

**Market sophistication and market awareness are distinct: sophistication comes from being pitched repeatedly; awareness comes from having access to competitive alternatives.** Understanding this distinction is critical for updating your messaging in competitive markets.

> Sophistication just means simply put I've just been pitched a lot of times I'm now aware of how other people with their processes are what their unique mechanisms are what their pricing is I get to make choices at a higher level as a result of having that level of sophistication as a consumer okay and as a business it takes a different type of marketing to sell to that type of consumer market awareness is as the consumer okay becomes that much more sophisticated they also are just hyper aware they've got leverage because they've got choices.

**Warm audiences (existing followers, email list, website visitors) outperform cold audiences for webinar launches because they have higher show rates (30-40% vs 10-15%) and are more forgiving of mistakes.** It's far less risky to test webinars with warm audiences first.

> It is a far greater risk to do a cold paid advertising webinar, comparatively to doing it to your organic audience and or your warm paid advertising audience.

### Customer Selection

**Actively deter Type 3 and Type 4 buyers (the cynical, long-sales-cycle prospects) rather than try to convert them.** Their tendency toward inaction, reputation damage, and support burden makes them worse than worthless. Focus exclusively on attracting buyer types 1 and 2.

> What you want to attempt to do is you want to attempt to try to start considering how to actively deter buyer types number three and four and how to exclusively attract buyer types number one and two.

**Non-buyers and haters still talk about you and spread awareness.** Leverage this by creating polarizing content that generates opinions, as the unsold audience often gets converted through hearing these discussions from others.

> The people who are non-buyers, they still talk about stuff that lead people who are on the right side of the spectrum or the unsolved side of the spectrum who can get knocked into the soul side of the spectrum through data and key opinions, key content

### Entrepreneurial Mindset

**New business owners have inherently poor survival odds regardless of paid vs. organic—success depends on endurance, financial backing, and individual traits, not the channel chosen.** The paid vs. organic debate is largely irrelevant when foundational survival odds are low.

> Business owners that are brand new to the game are like little sea turtles. They have the highest probability of death.. I don't think in any scenario there's a higher probability or a lower probability that they're going to do something better or for worse. They're [ __ ] either way

**High investment in learning creates a paradox: you get loaded with too many action items, so the real skill is filtering to the 1-2 strategies in your 'pocket of certainty' rather than trying to implement everything.** Information overload is a prioritization problem.

> For the people that have the courage to invest into themselves. They'll get all kinds of information. They'll have so many strategies to take action on. They'll have so many things they can do that load them up with the possibility to make more revenue

**The future of info will reward innovation and adaptability above all else.** Those who don't innovate and stay rigid will get left behind as the market consolidates to top performers.

> I think the info space will reward innovation like never before. I think you have to be extremely creative and extremely adaptable at all times if you're not adaptable and if you're not innovative you will get left behind.
