# Marketing Email Swipes

**Goal:** One-off broadcast / promotional / newsletter emails. Single-send, not part of a sequence.

**Trigger:** Manual send to a segmented list.

**Recommended framework:** Varies by email type — see each template.

Four swipe templates here, each for a different job. Pick the one that matches your goal.

---

## 1. Structure Email (Problem → Solution)

**Goal:** Surface a structural gap in the reader's business and offer a call as the fix.

**Framework:** PAS

```
Subject: Are you lacking structure?

Hey,

I don't mean on the "[product / delivery]" side of your business. I mean on the GROWTH side.

You have two sides to your business:

Product: [list 3–4 components — e.g. "fulfillment, customer service, quality control, retention"]
Growth: [list 3–4 components — e.g. "lead gen, sales, follow-up, referrals"]

Most likely you have great structure on the Product side.

But where most [audience] have serious problems is on the GROWTH side.

If you can confidently say you have a structured, step-by-step process for each growth system above, click "Unsubscribe."

On the other hand —

If you do NOT have a step-by-step process for:

1. [Growth element 1]
2. [Growth element 2]
3. [Growth element 3]

Then you need to book a call right now: [Link]

[Testimonial — verbatim quote + name + result]

— [Sender Name]
```

>>> Why this works: The "click Unsubscribe" line is a confidence move that filters the list to people who actually need help. The structural framing (Product vs. Growth) gives the reader a new mental model — usually a key buying trigger.

>>> Caveat: Only use this once or twice a year. If you send "click unsubscribe" emails monthly, the list catches on and the move loses its punch.

## 2. Case Study Email

**Goal:** Drive a CTA off a single named client result.

**Framework:** BAB

```
Subject: What would [specific result] do for you?

Hey,

I thought you'd want to see this — you can probably relate to [Client Name].

[1–2 sentences about their before state. Industry, size, struggle. From real client.]

Now, [X timeframe] later: [specific impressive result with numbers].

[Video link OR screenshot of result]

Let me know if you have any questions.

— [Sender Name]

P.S. If you haven't booked a call: [Link]
```

>>> Why this works: Short. Anchored on a real result. The reader does the imagination work themselves — the subject line plants "what would $X do for you?" and they answer it without you having to push.

>>> Don't have a video / screenshot of the result? Use a verbatim text testimonial with the client's full name. Don't fake it with stock photos or anonymized names — trust collapses fast.

## 3. Objection Handling Email

**Goal:** Address the #1 reason a segment hasn't bought, and convert the on-the-fence subscribers.

**Framework:** PAS

```
Subject: It's time to fire these b*stards

Hey,

[Company / Brand] is NOT a [category they might expect — e.g. "another marketing agency"].

If you're sick of [common frustration with the alternative], keep reading — because this is going to resonate.

How many [service providers / coaches / agencies / programs] have you worked with since you started?

Let me share a SECRET:

[Key insight about the underlying problem with how the industry handles this. One sharp sentence.]

The ONLY thing stopping you from [desired outcome] is the KNOWLEDGE to do it on your own.

Think about it — if you had the knowledge to:

- [Skill 1]
- [Skill 2]
- [Skill 3]

You would have FULL CONTROL over [area of business].

If you're interested in taking that control: [Link]

— [Sender Name]
```

>>> Why this works: The subject line is polarizing — it self-selects the audience. The "fire these b*stards" frame names the enemy (bad service providers) without naming names. Reader feels validated and primed to act.

>>> Caveat: Tone-check this against your client's brand voice. It works for direct, contrarian brands (think Gary Vee's audience). It doesn't work for buttoned-up enterprise B2B.

## 4. Social Proof Email

**Goal:** Drop a single big-result testimonial / case to drive a CTA.

**Framework:** Direct + BAB

```
Subject: Fwd: $[Amount] [result] in [timeframe]

You won't believe this.

[Client Name] used to [old situation — specific].

In their FIRST [timeframe] with our program, they [specific impressive result].

The best part is they're finally [emotional benefit — what their life is like now].

[Video / testimonial embed]

This isn't out of the ordinary — most of our clients are [typical result range].

Best,
[Sender Name]

P.S. Book your call here: [Link]
```

>>> Why this works: "Fwd:" subject implies inside info. Specific numbers and timeframe make it credible. The "this isn't out of the ordinary" line normalizes the result without explicitly making promises.

>>> The result must be real. The "Fwd:" framing is one of the highest-trust-burn moves if it's caught as fake. Use it only with real wins you can show in video or screenshot.

## Customization checklist (all four)

- [ ] One CTA per email — same destination top and bottom if you use both.
- [ ] Subject line passes the rules in `subject-lines.md` (6–10 words, no spam triggers).
- [ ] Sender name matches what the list expects.
- [ ] Testimonial / case study uses **real** name, **real** numbers, **real** result.
- [ ] Reply-to is monitored — broadcasts surface real prospects in replies.
- [ ] Send time: 3 pm recipient timezone, Tuesday or Friday for sales.

---

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                                         | Fix                                                       |
|-----------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Sending the same swipe to the whole list every month            | Rotate. Each one works once a quarter, not weekly.        |
| "Fwd:" / "Re:" subjects without real context                    | Burns trust if detected. Use sparingly, with real reasons. |
| Generic "amazing results" instead of specific numbers           | Specifics convert. "Lost 24 lbs" beats "lost weight" every time. |
| All four emails sent in one week                                | Space them out. The list needs breathing room between asks. |
| Vague CTA ("learn more")                                        | Action-driven CTA always. "Book my call" / "Apply now" / "Get instant access". |
