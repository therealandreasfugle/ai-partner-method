# Google Business Profile

The biggest single lever on a local business getting found, and it is free.

For most local trades their Google listing brings in more work than their website
does. The website's job is partly to make the listing convert. Treat this as part
of the build, not an extra.

There are deeper SEO references in `skills/seo-local` and `skills/seo-maps`. This
page is what you actually do for a client.

---

## First, get access

Ask for this on the onboarding form. It is the `gbp` field.

**If they already have a profile:** they add you as a manager. They go to their
Business Profile, then Settings, then People and access, then Add, and put in
your email. Manager is enough, you never need owner.

**If they have not claimed it:** the listing usually still exists, Google made it
from other data. They claim it at https://business.google.com and verify, which
these days is often a video call or a video they record. That verification can
take a week or two, so start it on day one of the build, not at the end.

**If there is no listing at all:** create it. This is the easiest win you will
ever hand a client.

⚠️ Never create a second listing for a business that already has one. Duplicate
listings split their reviews and can get both suspended. If you find duplicates,
report them to Google rather than making a third.

---

## What to actually fill in

Do these in order. It takes about an hour.

**The name.** Exactly their real trading name. Nothing else.

⚠️ Do not stuff keywords into the name. "Smith Plumbing" is fine. "Smith Plumbing
Emergency Plumber Leeds 24 Hour" is a suspension waiting to happen and a
competitor can report it in two clicks.

**The category.** The primary category matters more than almost anything else on
the profile. Pick the most specific one that fits. Plumber, not Contractor. Then
add secondary categories for the other work they do.

**Service areas.** The towns they actually cover. Same list as the website, and
do not pad it.

**Hours.** Match the website exactly. Add the special hours for bank holidays and
Christmas, because a wrong "open now" costs them a job and earns a bad review.

**Phone.** The number they answer. Same one as the site.

**Website.** The new site. This is the link that sends their listing traffic to
you, so do it the day you launch.

**Services.** List every service with a short description. Most businesses leave
this empty and it is free space to say what they do.

**Description.** What they do, who for, where, and how long they have been at it.
Plain sentences. No sales language, Google shows this to people who are already
looking.

**Photos.** The single most neglected part. Real photos of real jobs, the van,
the team, the premises. Businesses with photos get contacted noticeably more than
ones without. Ask for twenty, use all of them, and add more every few months.

**Products, if it fits.** Some trades can list jobs as products with prices. Worth
ten minutes to check.

---

## Then keep it alive

- Post to it monthly. A finished job with a photo and two sentences is enough
- Answer every review, good and bad, within a few days
- Answer the questions people leave, and seed the obvious ones yourself
- Update the hours before every bank holiday

The review engine you built handles the asking. See `04-deliver/06-reviews`.

---

## What to check afterwards

- Search their business name on a phone and see the listing
- Search the main job plus the town, in an incognito window, and see where they
  come
- Click the website link on the listing and confirm it lands on the new site

⚠️ Rankings move for reasons nobody controls. Report what you did and what
happened. Never promise a client a position.
