# Frameworks

The structural patterns. Pick one based on the audience temperature and goal — don't mix them in a single email.

---

## PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution

**Use when:** the audience is already aware of their pain and looking for a fix.

**Structure:**

1. **Problem** — open with their pain, in their words. Not yours.
2. **Agitate** — amplify the emotional cost of *not* solving it.
3. **Solution** — your offer as the fix.

**Example skeleton:**

> *Subject: Why your last 3 launches stalled at $8k*
>
> You wrote good copy. You ran solid ads. The launch still flatlined at $8k and you don't know why.
>
> Every month it stays this way is another month watching peers double up while you're stuck refining the same funnel that isn't working.
>
> The piece you're missing isn't ads. It's a follow-up sequence that converts the 70% of leads who don't buy on day one.
>
> [CTA: book a call to map yours]

**When *not* to use:** cold audiences who don't know they have the problem yet. They need AIDA.

## AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

**Use when:** cold or semi-cold audience that needs to be hooked from scratch.

**Structure:**

1. **Attention** — pattern interrupt in line one. A question, a stat, a contrarian claim.
2. **Interest** — bridge to their world. Why this matters to them specifically.
3. **Desire** — paint the transformation. What life looks like once they have this.
4. **Action** — single, clear CTA.

**Example skeleton:**

> *Subject: 73% of fitness coaches make under $4k/mo*
>
> Three out of four coaches you follow on Instagram aren't breaking $4k a month. They're broke and playing the part.
>
> Here's why: the coaching industry trains you to deliver — not to sell. So the people with the best programs end up worst at marketing.
>
> Imagine running a coaching business where you wake up to 5 booked calls a week, all qualified, all ready to pay. No DM grind. No content treadmill. Just leads coming to you.
>
> [CTA: see how the 27% do it]

**When *not* to use:** hot audiences. They don't need the hook — they need the solution.

## BAB — Before, After, Bridge

**Use when:** selling a transformation. Especially good for testimonial-driven emails and case studies.

**Structure:**

1. **Before** — current frustrating state, in detail.
2. **After** — specific desired outcome. Numbers if you have them.
3. **Bridge** — how your offer gets them from B to A.

**Example skeleton:**

> *Subject: From 0 clients to 12 in 90 days*
>
> Sarah was running a yoga studio that had been losing money for 18 months. She'd tried Facebook ads, Instagram, partnerships with local gyms — nothing stuck. She was three months from closing.
>
> Today she has 12 monthly members, runs at 80% capacity, and is hiring her first instructor. Net revenue: $11k/mo.
>
> The change wasn't more marketing. It was a single referral system that turned every existing member into a recruiter — built in one weekend.
>
> [CTA: see if it works for your studio]

**When *not* to use:** when you don't have a real before/after story. Don't fake it. Use AIDA or PAS instead.

## The Email Matrix

**Use when:** you have segmentation data. This isn't an email *structure* — it's a *targeting* framework.

The principle: **context = more money**. Same email, sent to a segmented audience, converts 4–6x better than the same email sent to a generic list.

### How to apply it

1. **Identify segments** in your list (see `SOP.md` → "Segmentation" for 15 ways).
2. **Write a base email** using PAS, AIDA, or BAB.
3. **Customize the hook and CTA per segment.** Body can stay similar; intros and offers should match the segment.

### Example — same offer, three audiences

A consultant offering a "scale to $30k/mo" course.

**Segment A: solo consultants stuck at $5k/mo.**

> Subject: Why solo consultants get stuck at $5k
>
> *(Hook: their specific ceiling. Pain = trading time for money.)*

**Segment B: agency owners at $20k looking to scale to $50k.**

> Subject: The $20k → $50k systems gap
>
> *(Hook: their specific ceiling. Pain = team capacity, not lead gen.)*

**Segment C: course creators wanting recurring revenue.**

> Subject: Adding monthly revenue without launching another course
>
> *(Hook: their specific desire. Pain = launch fatigue.)*

Same product. Three open rates. Three conversion rates. The "Email Matrix" is the discipline of *never sending the generic version when you could send the targeted version*.

## Picking the right framework — decision tree

```
Is the audience aware of their pain?
├── Yes → Are you selling a transformation?
│   ├── Yes → BAB
│   └── No  → PAS
└── No (cold) → AIDA

Do you have segmentation data on this list?
├── Yes → Apply the Email Matrix on top of whichever framework you picked.
└── No  → Send the base email; start collecting segmentation data ASAP.
```

---

## What changes by framework — what doesn't

**Stays the same regardless of framework:**

- Subject line: 6–10 words.
- Single CTA per email.
- Mobile-first formatting (≤4 lines per paragraph).
- One credibility element minimum.
- Send timing (3 pm local, Tue/Fri for sales).

**Changes by framework:**

- Opening line.
- Order of emotional beats.
- Length of body (BAB tends longer, PAS tends shorter, AIDA varies).
- Subject line angle (AIDA = curiosity, PAS = pain, BAB = transformation).

## Framework misuse — common errors

| Error                                                    | Fix                                                       |
|----------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Using PAS on a cold list                                 | They don't know the pain yet. Switch to AIDA.             |
| BAB without a real before/after story                    | Don't fabricate. Switch to PAS or AIDA.                   |
| Mixing AIDA and PAS in one email                         | Pick one. Mixing dilutes both.                            |
| AIDA hook that doesn't bridge to interest                | The "A" hooked them but the "I" didn't bring them home. Re-write the second paragraph. |
| Generic broadcast when you have segmentation data       | Apply the Email Matrix. Send the targeted version.        |
