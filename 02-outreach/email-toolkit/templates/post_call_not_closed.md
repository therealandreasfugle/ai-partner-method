# Post-Call: Not Closed (3 emails)

**Goal:** Re-engage on-the-fence prospects and address their objections.

**Trigger:** Lead was on the call but didn't buy.

**Recommended framework:** PAS for Email 2 (objection handling), direct for #1 and #3.

---

## Email 1 — Recap

**Send:** Same day as the call (within 4 hours)
**Goal:** Reinforce the conversation while it's fresh. No pressure.

```
Subject: Thanks for your time today, [First Name]

Hey [First Name],

Really enjoyed our conversation today.

You mentioned [specific thing they said — pain point, goal, or constraint]. That stuck with me, and here's why:

[1–2 sentences connecting their situation to a relevant insight or proof point. Specific, not generic.]

Whatever you decide, here are [resource(s) — case study, article, video] that might help you think it through:
[Link]

No pressure either way. Just wanted to make sure you have what you need.

Questions? Just reply.

[Sender Name]
```

>>> Why this works: Mirrors their actual words back — proves you listened. The "no pressure" framing keeps the door open. The resource is a soft re-engagement hook.

---

## Email 2 — Objection Handling

**Send:** Day 2 (~24h after Email 1)
**Framework:** PAS
**Goal:** Address the #1 objection before they convince themselves out of it.

```
Subject: [Their #1 objection, framed as a question]

Hey [First Name],

I've been thinking about our conversation, and I want to address something head-on.

[State their objection in their words. E.g. "You said you weren't sure if you have the time." Or "You mentioned the price felt steep right now."]

Here's the thing most people in your position don't realize:

[The reframe. One specific, true insight. Not a slogan — a real perspective shift. Use a story or data point.]

[Insert a specific case study — Client X had the same concern, here's what happened.]

If [their objection] is the real reason you're hesitating, I get it — and I'd rather you not buy than buy and not be ready.

But if it's something else, hit reply and tell me what's actually going on. I'll give you a straight answer.

[Sender Name]
```

>>> Why this works: Naming the objection out loud disarms it. Most prospects expect to be sold harder — getting permission to *not* buy actually makes them more likely to buy. The reply ask surfaces the real objection (which is rarely the stated one).

>>> The reframe matters. *"You don't have time? Here's why you can't afford NOT to do it!"* doesn't work. *"You don't have time? Most clients in this seat were exactly there. The time they got back from the system in week 3 covered the program."* That works.

---

## Email 3 — Final Offer

**Send:** Day 5
**Framework:** Direct + light scarcity
**Goal:** Close the loop. Get a yes or a clear no.

```
Subject: Last note — [Program/Offer]

Hey [First Name],

Quick one.

I've kept your spot open since our call last week, but I'm closing out the [Month/Cohort] roster soon and I need to know whether to hold it or release it.

If you're in: [link to checkout / contract / next step]

If it's not the right fit right now: completely understand. Just reply "not now" and I'll close the file.

Either way, appreciate the conversation.

[Sender Name]

P.S. If something specific is in the way that we didn't address on the call, tell me what it is. I'd rather solve it than lose you to a problem I could have fixed.
```

>>> Why this works: Forces a binary. "I need to know" puts the ball in their court without manipulation. The P.S. opens one more door for the legit "I have a real concern" leads.

>>> Don't fake the urgency. If you don't actually close the cohort, don't say you do. Honesty compounds; manufactured scarcity collapses.

---

## Customization checklist

- [ ] Email 1's "specific thing they said" is a real quote from the call, not a generic guess.
- [ ] Email 2's objection matches the **most common** objection for this audience (not the rarest).
- [ ] Email 3's scarcity is real (cohort-based, capacity-based, or seasonal).
- [ ] All three emails reply-able. If they hit reply on any of them, a human responds.
- [ ] Resource link in Email 1 is **not** the sales page — it's value content.

---

## Common mistakes

| Mistake                                              | Fix                                                  |
|------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Email 2 attacks the objection head-on / dismisses it | Validate first ("I get it"), then reframe.           |
| Email 3 disguised as a "checking in" email           | Be direct. Pretending = lower close rate, not higher. |
| All 3 emails on day 1, 2, 3                          | Day 5 for #3. Gives them space to actually decide.   |
| Manufactured scarcity ("only 3 spots!" every week)   | Burns trust irreparably. One real "doors close" beats ten fake ones. |
| Sending Email 3 to people who already replied "no"   | Tag and exclude. Sending after a "no" damages relationships. |
